You made $100,000 trading this year. Congratulations!

Now send $22,000-$37,000 to the IRS, depending on your bracket. That leaves $63,000-$78,000 before your state takes its cut.

In a state with a 5% income tax, take off another $5,000.

🚨 Real Talk

Most traders focus on making money. Smart traders focus on keeping money.

Tax optimization isn't sexy. But it's 20-40% of your returns. Ignore it at your peril.

⚠️ Read This First

Everything in this lesson is US federal tax law, with contribution limits and brackets from the 2022 and 2023 tax years. Brackets, standard deductions and retirement limits are indexed every year, and your state adds its own rules on top.

If you trade outside the US, the concepts (holding periods, loss offsets, entity structure, deferred retirement accounts) usually have local equivalents, but none of the numbers here transfer. Nothing below is tax advice. Take it to a CPA who handles traders.

Short-Term vs. Long-Term Capital Gains

The IRS treats holding periods VERY differently.

Hold Less Than 1 Year

Tax rate: Ordinary income (22-37%)

$100K gain, held 364 days:
Tax: $22K-$37K (depending on bracket)
Net: $63K-$78K

Short-term = brutal tax hit. Most day traders pay this.

Hold More Than 1 Year

Tax rate: Long-term capital gains (0-20%)

$100K gain, held 366 days:
Tax: $0-$20K (depending on income)
Net: $80K-$100K

Savings vs. short-term: $17K+

Two extra days of holding = $17K saved. Do the math.

💡 The Aha Moment

364 days vs. 366 days = 17% tax savings on $100K gain.

If you're swing trading anyway, consider holding winners past 1 year.

Part 2: Trader Tax Status (TTS)

Qualify as a "Professional Trader" (Legally)

If you trade frequently enough, you can qualify for Trader Tax Status. Benefits are massive.

What Is TTS?

IRS criteria (all must be met):

  • Substantial activity: 720+ trades/year — a guideline from case law, not a number in the tax code
  • Regularity: Trade most days market is open
  • Continuity: Trading is primary income source
  • Intent: Profit from short-term price movements

If you qualify: Deduct business expenses (software, data, home office, etc.)

TTS Benefits 
Business Expense Deductions (realistic retail figures):
- Trading platform:      $200/mo x 12 = $2,400
- Market data feeds:     $200/mo x 12 = $2,400
- Home office (simplified method,
  300 sq ft x $5/sq ft, the cap)     = $1,500
- Computer equipment (Section 179)   = $5,000
- Education (courses, books)         = $3,000
                                       ------
Total deductions:                      $14,300
Tax savings: $14,300 x 32% =            $4,576

The actual-expense method can beat $1,500 on the
home office if you have the records for it. And a
professional data subscription can run far higher
than $200/mo - but if you are paying institutional
data rates, you are not reading this lesson.

TTS on a realistic retail expense base is worth a few thousand a year, not five figures. Still free money if you already qualify.

Part 3: Wash Sale Rule (The Trap)

You Sold at a Loss. But You Can't Deduct It.

This is where traders get burned.

Example:
Jan 1: Buy 100 SPY at $500 = $50,000
Jan 15: Sell 100 SPY at $480 = $48,000 (loss: -$2,000)
Jan 20: Buy 100 SPY at $485 (within 30 days)

Result: Loss DISALLOWED (wash sale rule)
Instead: -$2K added to cost basis of new position

You can't deduct the loss because you repurchased within 30 days.

3 Ways to Avoid

  1. Wait 31 days: sell at loss, wait 31 days, rebuy
  2. Buy a security that is not substantially identical: The IRS has never defined the phrase for ETFs. Swapping SPY for IVV or VOO — three funds tracking the same index — is a position many advisors consider aggressive, not a safe harbour. The conservative move is a fund tracking a different index (S&P 500 → total market, or → Russell 1000). Ask your CPA before you rely on this one.
  3. Use Mark-to-Market accounting: Eliminates wash sale rule entirely

MTM Election (Day Traders)

What it does: Treats all positions as "sold" on Dec 31 (paper gains/losses count)

Benefit 1: Eliminates the wash sale rule

Benefit 2 (the bigger one): Gains and losses become ordinary, not capital. That removes the $3,000-a-year cap on deducting net capital losses. A trader who loses $80,000 in a bad year deducts $80,000 instead of $3,000.

Downside: You lose long-term capital gains treatment permanently, and revoking the election needs IRS consent.

The deadline that catches people: The election for a given year must be filed by the due date of the prior year's return. To have MTM for 2026, you file by April 2025. Miss it and you wait a full year.

Good for day traders. Bad for position traders.

Part 4: Entity Structuring

S-Corp vs. LLC: What an Entity Actually Buys You

🚨 First, Kill a Myth

You will read everywhere that an S-Corp saves a trader self-employment tax. For a trader, there is no self-employment tax to save.

Trading your own capital produces capital gains, not self-employment income — and that stays true even with Trader Tax Status. A sole proprietor trader, a single-member LLC trader and an S-Corp trader all pay $0 of SE tax on their trading gains.

An S-Corp does not lower the rate on your trading profit. It does something else, and that something else is worth real money.

If you're making $200K+ trading, entity structure matters — but for a different reason than most articles claim.

Individual (Schedule D) 
$200K of short-term trading gains:
Federal income tax at ordinary rates
SE tax: $0 (capital gains are not earned income)

Simple. Cheap. Nothing to file beyond your 1040.

The catch: you have no EARNED income.
- No solo 401(k) contribution (needs compensation)
- No deductible health insurance premium
- No Social Security credits accruing
LLC (Pass-Through) 
$200K of short-term trading gains:
Federal income tax at ordinary rates
SE tax: $0 - identical to filing as an individual

A single-member LLC trading its own capital is a
disregarded entity. Your tax bill does not change
by one dollar.

What you get: liability separation and clean books.
What you save in tax: nothing.

An LLC is a legal wrapper, not a tax strategy. Anyone selling it to traders as a tax play is selling you something.

S-Corp (What It Actually Does)

The standard trader structure: a separate S-Corp management company charges the trading account a fee, and pays you a salary out of it. That salary is earned income — the thing your trading gains are not.

$200K trading profit, salary set at $60,000:

COST of the salary:
  Payroll tax 15.3% x $60,000        = $9,180
  (the employer half, $4,590, is deductible,
   so the real cost is about $7,700)

WHAT THE SALARY UNLOCKS:
  Solo 401(k) employee deferral       = $23,000
  Employer contribution, 25% of salary= $15,000
  Deductible health insurance premium = $12,000
                                        -------
  Income sheltered this year            $50,000
  At a 32% marginal rate                $16,000

Net: about $16,000 saved or deferred,
     minus about $7,700 of payroll tax,
     = roughly $8,300 a year ahead.

Note what this is and isn't. The health insurance premium is a permanent deduction. The 401(k) money is deferred, not forgiven — you pay tax on it when you withdraw. The point is that without earned income you could not put it in the account at all.

Deferral limits are indexed annually. The $23,000 employee figure is the 2024 limit; check the current year's number before you plan around it.

🎯 When to Consider S-Corp

Rough threshold: $150K+ of consistent net trading profit.

The structure costs roughly $6,000-$7,000 a year in CPA and admin fees, and adds a payroll tax bill you did not have before. Below six figures of steady profit, those fixed costs eat the benefit.

The real test is not your profit — it is whether you will actually fund the retirement account every year. If you will not, the salary is pure added cost and you should stay on Schedule D.

Part 4.5: Real-World Case Study

Rachel's $12,419 Tax Correction: Before & After

Trader: Rachel Thompson (composite example), 42, full-time derivatives trader from Chicago, IL
Timeframe: Tax Year 2022, filed unoptimized — then re-run with the structure she should have had
Trading Income: $195,000 net short-term gains
Filing Status: Single, Illinois resident (4.95% flat state tax)

⚠️ The Trap She Fell Into First

Rachel's first instinct was the one most traders have: she looked up her marginal bracket, saw 32%, multiplied $195,000 by it, and concluded she owed $62,400 in federal tax.

That is not how brackets work. The 32% applies only to the dollars above the bracket threshold. Every dollar below is taxed at 10%, 12%, 22% and 24% on the way up. Her actual federal bill was $38,488 — a 19.7% effective rate, not 32%.

Before you optimize anything, get this right. Half the "tax horror stories" traders tell each other are marginal rates mistaken for effective ones.

Column A: What She Actually Filed (2022, No Optimization)

Rachel traded SPX options full-time, averaging 8-12 trades per day — comfortably past the TTS activity guideline. She just never claimed it.

Rachel's 2022 Return As Filed — 2022 brackets, single filer

Short-term trading gains $195,000 Taxed at ordinary rates
Business expenses claimed $0 She spent $22,400 and deducted none of it — no TTS claimed
Standard deduction -$12,950 2022 single filer
Taxable income $182,050 —
Federal income tax $38,488 10/12/22/24/32% stacked, not 32% flat
Illinois state tax $9,532 4.95% of $195,000 less the $2,425 exemption
Self-employment tax $0 Trading gains are not earned income. This never applied.
TOTAL TAX $48,020 Effective rate: 24.6%
Kept $146,980 Retirement saved through the business: $0

Column B: The Same $195,000, Properly Structured

Same income, same year, same state. The only change is TTS plus an S-Corp management company paying her an $85,000 salary — which is what makes a solo 401(k) and a deductible health premium possible at all.

TTS business expenses -$22,400 Platform, data, home office, hardware, education — money she already spent

Employer half deducted by the entity -$6,503 Half of payroll tax is a business deduction

Retirement funded $43,750 $0 in Column A. This is the part nobody talks about.

🎯 The Honest Scorecard

Metric As filed Properly structured
Trading profit $195,000 $195,000
Total tax $48,020 $35,601
Effective rate 24.6% 18.3%
Tax difference — $12,419
Cost of the structure $0 -$6,700 (CPA $4,500 + admin $2,200)
Net cash ahead, year one — $5,719
Retirement space opened $0 $43,750/year

Read the last two rows together. The cash saving is $5,719 — real, but not life-changing. The thing that actually changes Rachel's life is the bottom row: a full-time trader with no earned income cannot put a dollar into a 401(k). The salary is what opens the door, and $43,750 a year compounding tax-deferred for twenty years is a different retirement.

And be clear about what the 401(k) money is: deferred, not forgiven. She pays income tax on it when she withdraws it. The bet is that her rate then is lower than 32% now, and that twenty years of untaxed compounding beats the difference. That is usually a good bet. It is still a bet.

🚨 What This Case Study Is Not

It is not a promise of a 6-point drop in your effective rate. Rachel's numbers come from one filing status, one state, one income level and one year's brackets. Change any of those and the arithmetic changes.

What transfers is the method: compute your effective rate rather than your marginal one, deduct what you are already spending, and understand that the entity's job is to manufacture earned income — not to make your trading profit taxed at a lower rate. Nothing does that.

Part 5: Tax-Loss Harvesting

Turning Losers Into Winners

December strategy: Realize losses to offset gains.

Year-end positions:
- SPY: +$50,000 gain (sell, lock gain)
- QQQ: -$15,000 unrealized loss (sell, realize loss)
- IWM: +$5,000 gain (sell)

Net taxable gain: $50K + $5K - $15K = $40K

Tax saved: the loss x YOUR marginal rate.
At 32%: $15,000 x 32% = $4,800

Action: after 31 days, rebuy QQQ.

The cost nobody prices in: for those 31 days you are
FLAT. If QQQ runs 6% while you wait, the move cost you
more than the $4,800 you saved. Harvest losses when you
wanted out anyway, or when you have a non-identical
substitute you are happy to hold.

And note the window runs both ways: 30 days BEFORE the
sale as well as after. Buying more on the way down and
then selling the original lot triggers it too.

🎓 Key Takeaways

  • Hold > 1 year for long-term gains (0-20% vs. 22-37%)
  • Know your EFFECTIVE rate, not your marginal one — they are not close
  • Trader Tax Status: deduct what you already spend (a few thousand a year)
  • Wash sales: wait 31 days, and treat "similar security" as an unsettled question
  • Trading gains carry NO self-employment tax. An entity does not change your rate.
  • An S-Corp salary buys earned income, which is what funds a solo 401(k)
  • Tax-loss harvest in December: offset gains with losses, and price the 31 days flat

📝 Practice Exercise

Calculate Your Tax Efficiency Optimization Strategy

  1. Estimate your current tax burden
    • Calculate YTD trading P&L (gains - losses)
    • Identify short-term vs. long-term gains
    • Apply tax rates: Short-term (22-37%), Long-term (0-20%)
    • Calculate estimated tax owed
  2. Analyze wash sale exposure
    • Review last 60 days of trades
    • Identify: Did you sell at a loss and rebuy within 30 days?
    • Calculate disallowed losses (these get added to cost basis)
  3. Evaluate Trader Tax Status eligibility
    • Count trades this year (720+ is the working guideline)
    • Calculate frequency (trade most days market is open?)
    • List deductible expenses: Platform, data, home office, education
    • Calculate potential tax savings if you qualify
  4. Plan December tax-loss harvesting
    • List open positions with unrealized losses
    • Calculate benefit of realizing losses (loss amount × tax rate)
    • Create plan: Sell losers, wait 31 days, rebuy (or buy similar asset)
  5. Determine if entity structure makes sense
    • If profit > $150K/year and steady: research an S-Corp with a CPA who handles traders
    • Do NOT compute an "SE tax saving" — there is none on trading gains. Compute instead: (retirement contribution + health premium) × your marginal rate, minus payroll tax on the salary.
    • Compare that to the real cost: CPA fees plus entity admin, roughly $6K-$7K/year

Goal: Build a plan on your own numbers rather than on someone else's headline figure — and know which parts are permanent savings and which are deferrals.

⚡ Quick Wins for Tomorrow (Click to expand)
  1. Check positions approaching 366 days — Any position held 365 days? Hold one more day for long-term rates. At the top bracket that is 20% instead of 37%, so 17 points of the gain stays with you.
  2. Harvest losses before year-end — Losing positions? Sell before Dec 31 to offset gains. Wait 31 days to avoid wash sale rule if re-entering.
  3. Count your trades for TTS eligibility — The working guideline is 720+ trades a year, roughly 4 a day across at least 180 trading days. There is no statutory number; this is what tax courts and practitioners have converged on.

🎮 Test Your Understanding (No Pressure)

Question 1: You sell SPY at a loss on Jan 15. When can you rebuy to avoid wash sale?

A) Jan 30 (15 days later)
B) Feb 16 (31+ days later)
C) Immediately (no wait needed)
D) Next tax year only
Correct: B. The wash-sale rule disallows the loss if you buy a substantially identical security within 30 days either side of the sale, so the first clear repurchase is day 31 after — mid-February for a 15 January sale. A stops the clock early and the loss is disallowed, which is the expensive version of this mistake. C is the same error with no wait at all, and D is stricter than the rule requires, parking capital for no reason.
