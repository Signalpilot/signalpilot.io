Multi-Timeframe Confluence: The Highest-Probability Setups

Single-timeframe analysis is amateur hour. Professionals stack timeframes to find confluence—when multiple timeframes align in same direction. These are the highest-probability, lowest-risk setups in trading.

💸 The $8,800 Single-Timeframe Round-Trip

In 2019, a day trader saw a "perfect" bull flag breakout on the 5-minute TSLA chart and went long 2,000 shares at $240—a $480,000 position with a 1% stop at $237.60, so $4,800 at risk. The trade looked flawless on the 5-minute chart.

What he missed: On the daily chart, TSLA was hitting major resistance at $242 (previous swing high from 3 months ago). On the weekly chart, TSLA was overbought (RSI 75) and forming a bearish engulfing candle.

Result: TSLA spiked to $243 (+1.25%), then reversed violently, took out his stop at $237.60 for -$4,800, and was at $235 (-2%) by 2 PM. The higher timeframes were not telling him to buy the breakout—they were telling him to sell into $242. Trimming there would have paid +$4,000. The gap between the trade he took and the trade the chart was offering is $8,800 on the same 2,000 shares.

Lesson: Lower-timeframe setups MUST align with higher-timeframe context, or they're coin flips.

Part 1: The Multi-Timeframe Framework

The Three Timeframe Rule

Concept: Analyze 3 timeframes: Higher (trend), Trading (entry), Lower (precision)

Trading Style Higher TF (Trend) Trading TF (Entry) Lower TF (Precision)
Scalping 15-min 5-min 1-min
Day Trading Daily 15-min / 1-hour 5-min
Swing Trading Weekly Daily 4-hour
Position Trading Monthly Weekly Daily

Role of Each Timeframe

Higher TF: Determines bias (long or short). Only trade WITH higher TF trend.

Trading TF: Identifies setup (support/resistance, pattern, breakout)

Lower TF: Fine-tunes entry and stop placement (reduce risk)

🔑 Golden Rule: Never fight higher timeframe. If daily is downtrend, don't take longs on 15-min chart (countertrend = low probability). Only trade pullbacks in direction of higher TF.

Part 2: Multi-Timeframe Trend Analysis

Identifying Trend on Each Timeframe

Uptrend: Higher highs + higher lows (price > 50 EMA AND 200 EMA)

Downtrend: Lower highs + lower lows (price < 50 EMA AND 200 EMA)

Range: No clear direction (price between 50 and 200 EMA, choppy)

Confluence Matrix Example (Day Trading)

Example: AAPL Multi-Timeframe Analysis

Weekly (Higher TF): Uptrend (price above 50/200 EMA, making higher highs)

Daily (Trading TF): Pulled back to daily 50 EMA support (bullish)

1-Hour (Lower TF): Formed bullish engulfing candle at daily support

Confluence:

  • ✅ Weekly trend = up (trade longs only)
  • ✅ Daily at key support (50 EMA)
  • ✅ 1-hour reversal signal (bullish engulfing)

Setup: Buy on 1-hour chart, stop below daily 50 EMA, target weekly resistance

Probability: HIGH (all timeframes aligned)

Divergence = WARNING

Scenario: Weekly uptrend, but daily forming lower highs (bearish divergence)

Interpretation: Higher TF trend weakening → reduce position sizes, tighten stops

Action: Wait for daily to confirm reversal OR weekly to reclaim bullish structure

Part 2.5: The $80,720 Lesson—Brian's Single-Timeframe Disaster

Brian's $80,720 Single-Timeframe Disaster: When 5-Minute "Perfection" Hits Institutional Walls

Brian Walsh (composite example) — Day trader, $180K account.

June 2024: Brian was a pure 5-minute chart trader. Momentum breakouts with $80K-$120K intraday positions on a 1% stop, so roughly $800-$1,200 of risk per trade, and about five trades a day. Motto: "5-minute chart tells me everything I need."

By August 2024: 200 trades, 76 winners (38%). Average win $700, average loss $1,080 — $53,200 of gross profit against $133,920 of gross loss, a profit factor of 0.40 and a net loss of -$80,720 (-44.8%). Account: $180,000 → $99,280.

🚨 What Brian Learned The Hard Way

"My stops weren't random—they hit at the EXACT points where big money defends key levels. I'd see a perfect 5-minute breakout and go all-in, only to get stopped out at major daily or weekly levels I never checked."

— Brian Walsh, composite example

The Pattern: He marked up all 124 losers afterwards. In 79 of them—about four in five—the stop sat on the far side of a daily or weekly level he had never opened. The 5-minute setup was not wrong; it was aimed at a wall.

The Institutional Walls He Hit

Trade #1 (TSLA Jun 18): Perfect 5-min breakout at $178.60—560 shares, $100,000, stop $176.81. Missed: daily resistance at $179 (rejected 3× in 2 weeks), a 4H double top, weekly RSI 68. TSLA tagged $179.50, was rejected, and ran back to $175.50. Stopped at $176.81. Loss: -$1,002.

Trade #2 (NVDA Jul 9): Perfect 5-min breakdown at $117.90—933 shares short, $110,000, stop $119.08. Missed: the daily 50 EMA at $117.50 (defended 7×), a 4H hammer, a weekly uptrend. NVDA bounced to $122. Stopped at $119.08. Loss: -$1,101.

The same shape repeated across the other 198 trades. Total: -$80,720.

The Rebuild: January-June 2025

New Process (4 timeframes):

  1. Weekly = Trend bias (only trade WITH weekly trend)
  2. Daily = Support/resistance zones (don't fight these)
  3. 4-hour = Confirmation signal
  4. 5-min = Precise entry timing

RULE: All timeframes must align or skip the trade.

💡 Brian's Lesson

Multi-timeframe analysis isn't optional—it's the foundation.

  • 2-minute check before every trade: Weekly → Daily → 4H
  • If ANY timeframe shows resistance, skip the long
  • If ANY timeframe shows support, skip the short

A two-minute higher-timeframe check cut his trade count by four fifths. That is how it works: not by improving trades, but by stopping the ones that the higher timeframe was already arguing against.

Case Study Quiz: Brian lost $80,720 (-44.8%) over 200 trades in 2 months with a 38% win rate, despite seeing "perfect" setups. He was a pure 5-minute chart day trader running $80K-$120K positions on 1% stops. His motto: "5-minute chart tells me everything I need." B) His stops were too tight at 1%, so ordinary intraday noise took him out of trades that would otherwise have worked
C) He traded through the low-volume lunch session, when 5-minute breakouts fail far more often than they do at the open

A trader with oversized positions and tight stops still wins about half the time if his entries are neutral. Brian's were not neutral: they were pointed at levels other people were defending. Part 3: Multi-Timeframe Support & Resistance

Stacked Support = Strongest Levels

Concept: When multiple timeframes have support/resistance at same price, level is HIGH-CONVICTION

Example: SPY pulling back into $450

  • Weekly: the shelf it broke out from in the prior leg = $450
  • Daily: rising 200 EMA = $452
  • 4-Hour: 61.8% retracement of the last swing = $449

Result: three independent methods land inside a $3 band—0.7% wide. Note what makes that tradeable: not that a bounce is guaranteed, but that the invalidation is a single tight level. Below $448 all three are gone at once, so the stop is small and unambiguous. Confluence buys you a cheap "I was wrong", not a high hit rate.

One caution: a level is only confluence if the methods are independent. A daily 50 EMA and a 4-hour 200 EMA in the same spot are close to the same calculation on the same data—two readings of one thing, not two votes.

How to Find Stacked Levels

Step-by-Step Process

Step 1: Mark weekly S/R (major swing highs/lows, 50/200 MA)

Step 2: Mark daily S/R (recent swing highs/lows, 20/50/200 EMA)

Step 3: Mark 4-hour S/R (intraday structure)

Step 4: Look for overlap (where 2-3 timeframes converge within 0.5-1%)

Step 5: Those levels = highest-probability trade zones

Part 4: Multi-Timeframe Entry Strategies

Strategy #1: Pullback to Higher TF Support

Setup:

  • Weekly: Uptrend
  • Daily: Pullback to weekly 50 EMA (support)
  • 1-Hour: Reversal pattern (hammer, engulfing, double bottom)

Entry: Buy when 1-hour confirms reversal (break above prior candle high)

Stop: Below weekly 50 EMA (risk defined by higher TF level)

Target: Weekly resistance or prior swing high

Strategy #2: Breakout Confirmation

Setup:

  • Daily: Consolidation at resistance for 10+ days
  • 4-Hour: Forming ascending triangle (higher lows)
  • 15-Min: Volume surge + breakout above daily resistance

Entry: Buy on 15-min breakout confirmation (close above resistance + volume)

Stop: Below 4-hour ascending triangle support

Target: Measured move (height of daily consolidation)

Strategy #3: Lower TF Rejection at Higher TF Level

Setup:

  • Weekly: Downtrend
  • Daily: Rally to weekly 200 EMA resistance
  • 1-Hour: Shooting star / bearish engulfing at weekly 200 EMA

Entry: Short when 1-hour breaks below shooting star low

Stop: Above weekly 200 EMA (tight, defined by rejection)

Target: Weekly support or prior swing low

Part 4.5: Advanced Multi-Timeframe Indicator Confluence

Combining Indicators Across Timeframes

Don't just check price action on multiple timeframes—check INDICATORS too. When RSI, MACD and the moving averages all point the same way across three or more timeframes, you have far fewer ways to be surprised. Read that as a narrower set of outcomes, not a higher hit rate: the value is that a trade which disagrees with four independent readings is one you can skip without regret.

RSI Multi-Timeframe Confluence

Example: RSI Oversold on 3 Timeframes

Setup (illustrative, SPY):

  • Weekly RSI: 68 (upper half — the weekly uptrend is intact and has not yet tipped into a reading you would fade)
  • Daily RSI: 28 (OVERSOLD)
  • 4-Hour RSI: 22 (EXTREMELY OVERSOLD)
  • 1-Hour RSI: 18 (PANIC OVERSOLD)

Price Action:

  • Weekly: Uptrend (higher lows)
  • Daily: Pulled back to 50 EMA
  • 4-Hour: Hammer candle at support

Confluence Signal:

  • ✅ Weekly trend bullish
  • ✅ Daily, 4-hour, 1-hour ALL oversold
  • ✅ Price at daily 50 EMA support
  • ✅ 4-hour reversal candle

Trade: Buy SPY, stop below 4-hour low

Result: SPY rallies 3.2% over the next four days and the three lower-timeframe RSIs normalise back into the 50-60 band.

Why This Works—and when it doesn't: Simultaneous oversold readings on the fast timeframes describe a short-term extreme, and in an intact higher-timeframe uptrend those tend to resolve upward. But oversold is not a buy signal on its own: in a downtrend the daily RSI can sit under 30 for weeks while price keeps falling. The weekly trend is what turns the reading into a trade, which is why it is the first line of the setup and not the last.

MACD Divergence Across Timeframes

Setup (illustrative, AAPL bearish divergence):

  • Weekly MACD: Topping (histogram declining)
  • Daily MACD: Bearish crossover (MACD line crossed below its signal line)
  • 4-Hour MACD: Bearish divergence (price higher high, MACD lower high)

Price Action:

  • Weekly: pushing back to $185, the prior high, but the MACD histogram is smaller than it was on that high
  • Daily: Double top forming at $185
  • 4-Hour: Lower highs on MACD while price making equal highs

Warning Signal:

  • ⚠️ All 3 timeframes showing momentum divergence
  • ⚠️ Weekly MACD declining = higher TF momentum dying
  • ⚠️ Daily crossover = short-term reversal signal
  • ⚠️ 4-hour divergence = immediate weakness

Trade: Close longs, wait for daily to confirm breakdown before shorting

Result: AAPL rolls over from $185 to $172 over the next two weeks (-7.0%). Read the divergence for what it is: momentum confirming less than price, which is a reason to stop paying up and to tighten risk. It is not a short signal. Divergences can run for months in a strong trend, and the number of traders who have shorted a "clear" weekly divergence into a further 20% rally is not small. The trade in the box is closing longs—not opening shorts.

Moving Average Alignment (EMA Cloud)

When price is above ALL major moving averages on ALL timeframes = strongest bullish setup

Perfect EMA Alignment Trade (illustrative)

Weekly Chart:

  • Price @ $125 > Weekly 21 EMA ($118) > Weekly 50 EMA ($108)
  • All weekly EMAs in bullish order (21 > 50)

Daily Chart:

  • Price @ $125 > Daily 20 EMA ($123) > Daily 50 EMA ($120) > Daily 200 EMA ($112)
  • All daily EMAs in bullish order

4-Hour Chart:

  • Price @ $125 > 4H 20 EMA ($124.50) > 4H 50 EMA ($123.80)
  • EMAs rising (not flat or declining)

Confluence:

  • ✅ Price above ALL EMAs on weekly, daily, and 4-hour
  • ✅ ALL EMAs in bullish order (shorter > longer)
  • ✅ ALL EMAs rising (not flat)

Strategy:

  • Buy pullbacks to 4-hour 20 EMA (first support)
  • Stop below daily 20 EMA (second line of defense)
  • Hold until weekly structure breaks

Result:

  • Price runs from $125 to $140 in 3 weeks (+12%)
  • Each pullback to the 4-hour 20 EMA holds (5 opportunities)
  • The trend stays intact until the weekly 21 EMA breaks (exit signal)

Key Insight: When price is above every moving average on every timeframe, the trend is doing the work for you: buy dips to first support (4H 20 EMA), stop below second support (daily 20 EMA), hold until weekly structure breaks.

Now the honest half. NVDA actually had exactly this alignment in early July 2024 — price above every EMA on the weekly, daily and 4-hour, all of them rising. It then fell from around $135 on 10 July to roughly $98 by 7 August: about -27% in a month, with the daily 20 EMA and then the daily 50 EMA cut through on the way down. Perfect alignment is a description of the past, not a forecast. It tells you which side to take and where the trend is invalidated. It does not tell you the trend will continue, and the setups that look most flawless are the ones where traders are most likely to skip the stop.

Volume Confirmation Across Timeframes

Don't just check if current candle has volume—check if volume is increasing across multiple timeframes.

Timeframe Volume Check Bullish Signal
Weekly Current week vs 4-week avg Above average (accumulation)
Daily Today vs 20-day avg 1.5× or higher (breakout)
4-Hour Current bar vs prev 10 bars 2× or higher (surge)
15-Min Entry candle vs hour avg Above average (confirmation)

Perfect Volume Confluence Example:

  • Weekly volume: 125% of 4-week average (institutions accumulating)
  • Daily volume: 180% of 20-day average (breakout day)
  • 4-hour volume: 220% of recent average (surge)
  • 15-min entry candle: Above hourly average (confirmation)

Result: Volume expanding on every timeframe in the same direction says the move is being paid for rather than drifted into, which is what separates a breakout from a poke. Treat it as a filter that removes obvious false breakouts, not as a probability you can put a number on — anyone quoting you a precise follow-through rate for "volume confluence" has fitted it to a sample you cannot see.

One mechanical caution: mid-week, the weekly bar is incomplete. Comparing a two-day-old weekly bar to a 4-week average will read as "low volume" every Tuesday. Either pro-rate it or wait for the week to close.

Quiz: Test Your Understanding

Q1: Weekly = uptrend, daily = downtrend, 1-hour = uptrend. Should you take a long trade on 1-hour?

Show Answer

Answer: MAYBE, but risky. Weekly uptrend is bullish, but daily downtrend creates headwind. Best case: This is daily pullback within weekly uptrend (wait for daily to confirm reversal). Worst case: Weekly trend reversing (avoid until daily confirms). Size small or wait for daily to align bullish.

Q2: Price at $100. Weekly 200 MA = $100, daily support = $100, 4-hour 50 EMA = $100. What's the trade?

Show Answer

Answer: It depends on the weekly trend, which the question does not give you—and that omission is the point. In a weekly uptrend this is the setup: wait for a 15-min or 1-hour reversal (hammer, double bottom), buy, stop just below $100. In a weekly downtrend the same cluster is a level to sell rallies into, not to buy. What the stacking actually buys you either way is a tight, unambiguous invalidation: below $100 all three methods fail at once, so you find out you are wrong cheaply. That is the edge—not a "high probability bounce".

Q3: Price has poked above daily resistance, but the daily bar has not closed above it and the 15-min chart shows lower highs since the poke on shrinking volume. Is the breakout valid?

Show Answer

Answer: Probably not yet. First, a wording trap worth killing: every timeframe shows the same price, so "the daily broke out but the 15-min hasn't" is impossible. What differs is structure—whether the break is being held. Here it isn't: an unclosed daily bar above the level plus lower highs and falling volume on the 15-min is the signature of a poke being sold, which is exactly what a stop run above an obvious level looks like. Wait for a daily close above resistance, or for the 15-min to build higher lows above it. If instead price falls back inside the range, the failed break itself becomes a short setup back toward the other side.

Practical Checklist

Before Every Trade:

  • Check higher TF trend (weekly for swing, daily for day trading): Only trade WITH trend
  • Identify trading TF setup (pullback to support, breakout, reversal pattern)
  • Use lower TF for entry precision (confirm reversal or breakout on 1-hour/15-min)
  • Mark stacked S/R levels (where 2-3 timeframes converge within 1%)
  • Check where in the session you are: the 10:00-11:30 and 14:00-16:00 windows carry the volume, the lunch hours carry the chop

⚡ Quick Wins for Tomorrow (Click to expand)

Don’t overwhelm yourself. Start with these 3 actions:

  1. Add the higher timeframe to your chart before every entry this week — you are checking one thing: does the trade agree with it, or fight it?
  2. Re-run your last ten losers through the three-timeframe check — count how many were countertrend on the higher timeframe. That count is the size of the problem.
  3. Write the stack down for one instrument — the weekly, daily and 4-hour levels that overlap. Those are the levels worth waiting for.

Key Takeaways

  • Three timeframe rule: Higher (trend), Trading (entry), Lower (precision)
  • Always trade WITH higher TF trend (countertrend = low probability)
  • Stacked S/R = highest conviction levels (weekly + daily + 4-hour overlap)
  • Entry requires lower TF confirmation (don't buy daily support until 1-hour confirms reversal)
  • Time-of-day matters: the volume sits in the first and last two hours; midday breakouts have less behind them
  • Streamline MTF analysis: Omnideck displays all indicators across multiple timeframes on one screen—no more tab-switching

Multi-timeframe confluence works mostly by subtraction: HTF bias, MTF structure, LTF entry is a filter that removes the trades aimed at levels other people are defending. Expect it to cut your trade count more than it lifts your win rate on the trades that survive—which, as Brian's numbers show, is where the money actually comes from.
