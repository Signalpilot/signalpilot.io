Lesson 43 introduced basic intermarket correlations (stocks vs bonds, dollar vs gold). This advanced lesson teaches you to build a complete intermarket framework—mapping causality chains, spotting regime shifts, and reading the context that equities have not priced yet. Not prediction. Context, usually a few sessions early, and a filter that keeps you out of trades every other market is arguing against.

🎯 What You'll Learn

By the end of this lesson, you'll be able to:

  • Intermarket relationships: Stocks/Bonds (inverse, but regime-dependent), USD/Commodities (inverse), Copper/Stocks (sometimes leading, and this lesson shows a case where it wasn't)
  • Divergences signal change: All relationships breaking = regime shift
  • Framework: Monitor 5+ intermarket relationships → When 3+ diverge, expect volatility
  • Honest limits: these relationships are regime-dependent, they break for months at a time, and nobody can give you a reliable hit rate for them

Part 1: The Intermarket Causality Chain

How Assets Drive Each Other

Primary driver → Secondary effect → Equity impact

Example Chain: Fed cuts rates → Dollar weakens → Commodities rally → Inflation expectations rise → Growth stocks underperform (higher discount rate)

Intermarket causality chains give you context equities have not priced yet. Read them as a sequence with loose, variable lags—not as a clock.

Key Causality Chains

Chain #1: Dollar → Commodities → Inflation → Equities

Mechanism:

  • Dollar falls (DXY down) → Commodities priced in USD rise (oil, copper, gold)
  • Commodity prices rise → Input costs increase → Inflation accelerates
  • Inflation rises → Fed tightens → Equity valuations compress

Leading indicator: DXY breaking support → expect commodity rally in 2-4 weeks → inflation data confirms in 1-2 months → equity pressure follows

Trade: If DXY breaks down, rotate OUT of long-duration tech (ARKK, QQQ) INTO commodities (XLE, XLB)

Chain #2: Credit Spreads → Risk Appetite → Equities

Mechanism:

  • High-yield spreads (HYG vs TLT) widen → Credit markets pricing in recession risk
  • Credit tightening → Companies can't refinance debt cheaply → Defaults rise
  • Risk-off → Equities sell off. The 1-3 month lead is the textbook figure; in practice credit led clearly in 2007 and early 2020, and lagged in 2018 and 2022

Leading indicator: the HYG/TLT ratio breaking down. Use the ratio rather than HYG alone—that is the whole point of the pairing. HYG carries duration, so it falls when rates rise even with spreads unchanged; dividing by TLT strips most of that out and leaves the spread story.

Trade: If credit spreads widening while SPX making new highs → SELL SPX (divergence = warning)

Chain #3: Yields → Growth vs Value → Sector Rotation

Mechanism:

  • 10-year yield rises → Discount rate for future earnings increases
  • Growth stocks (high PE, distant cash flows) hurt more than value (low PE, near-term cash flows)
  • Rotation: OUT of growth (QQQ, ARKK) INTO value (XLF, XLE, XLI)

On thresholds: you will see "10-year above 4% triggers growth-to-value" quoted as a rule. It isn't one. The 10-year sat above 4% for most of 2023 and 2024 while growth crushed value. What actually hurts long-duration equity is the speed of the move—a 60-80bp repricing in a few weeks—not the level it settles at.

Trade: If the 10-year adds 60bp or more in a month, expect the long-duration end of the equity market to take it worst: short QQQ / long XLF as a relative-value trade, sized for the fact that this relationship goes quiet for quarters at a time.

📊 Composite Example: Marcus and a $25,650 intermarket edge

Marcus Chen (composite example)

Marcus traded SPX solely based on price action and RSI. September-October 2023: Lost $8,400 on 14 long entries as SPX dropped from 4,550 → 4,200 (-7.7%). His indicators showed "oversold" repeatedly, yet price kept falling.

What he missed: 10Y Treasury yields surged from 4.2% → 4.9% (+70bp in 6 weeks). DXY rallied 3.8% to 107. Credit spreads (HYG/TLT) widened 12%. All three warned: "Risk-off, liquidity drain coming."

November 2023: Marcus added intermarket monitoring. By October 27th three things had turned while SPX was still making its low: the 10-year had tagged 5.02% intraday on the 23rd and failed to make a new high, DXY had rolled over from its 3 October peak near 107, and high-yield credit (HYG) had stopped falling. Note what did not confirm: copper was flat on the month. You rarely get all five.

His trade: Long SPX at 4,238 on November 1st—the day of the FOMC hold—on intermarket confluence: yields failing at 5%, dollar rolling over, credit stabilising. Held until December 15th. Exit: 4,719. Profit: +11.3% = $34,050 on a $300K position.

Net result (Sept-Dec): -$8,400 + $34,050 = $25,650 profit after learning intermarket analysis. Note the honest part: he did not catch the 27 October low. He paid 120 points for confirmation and still cleared the quarter.

"I was watching one chess piece (SPX) while institutions watched the whole board. It did not show me the bottom—I still paid up for confirmation. What it did was stop me buying the fourteen dips before it."

💸 The $2.8B Fund That Missed the Intermarket Signal

In early 2022, a macro hedge fund was heavily long SPX (bullish tech) based on earnings growth and low volatility. They ignored intermarket warnings.

What they missed:

  • 10Y Treasury yields: Broke out from 1.6% to 2.5% (Jan-Mar 2022) = major tightening signal
  • DXY (dollar): 95.7 in early January to 103.5 by late April, +8% = risk-off + liquidity drain
  • Credit spreads (HYG/TLT): Widening for 12 weeks straight = credit market stress

Result: SPX fell from 4,796 on 3 January to 3,666 on 16 June—a 23.6% drawdown—while yields soared. The fund was down 22% by mid-year. The rates signal was in the tape from the first week of January.

Lesson: In a tightening cycle, rates move first and equities argue with them for a while. That is not a law of markets—plenty of equity drawdowns start in equities—but when yields, credit and the dollar are all pointing the same way, an equity book positioned against all three is making a macro bet whether it means to or not.

Part 2: Advanced Correlation Analysis

Rolling Correlation (Dynamic Relationships)

Problem: Correlations are NOT static (SPX vs TLT correlation changes based on regime)

Regime SPX vs TLT Correlation Interpretation
Normal (2010-2019) -0.4 to -0.6 (inverse) Risk-on = stocks up, bonds down
Crisis (2008, 2020) -0.8 to -0.9 (strong inverse) Flight to safety extreme
QE Environment (2020-2021) +0.2 to +0.4 (POSITIVE!) Fed buying both → both rise

Key insight: When SPX and TLT BOTH rise (positive correlation) = Fed easing (bullish). When BOTH fall (correlation still positive but negative direction) = forced liquidation (panic).

Lead-Lag Relationships

Concept: One asset leads, another follows (with time lag)

The claim: copper leads equities by 1-3 months

  • Copper rises → Industrial demand strong → Global growth accelerating
  • Equities rally 1-3 months later as earnings reflect stronger economy

That is the mechanism, and it is a real one. What it is not is reliable: copper is now heavily driven by Chinese property and grid demand and by supply disruptions at a handful of mines, which have little to do with S&P earnings. The worked example below is a case where copper explicitly did not lead. Use it, but verify it on the move in front of you.

Trade: If copper breaks out (new 6-month high) while SPX is flat → a reason to look for a long, after you have checked whether the copper move is a demand story or a supply story. A mine outage tells you nothing about global growth.

Real-World Example: What Actually Turned First Before the 2023 Rally

Setup: October 2023 — SPX bottoms on the 27th. The textbook says copper leads. Here is what the tape actually did.

Timeline & Intermarket Signals:

Date Copper (HG) SPX DXY (Dollar) 10Y Yield Signal
Oct 3, 2023 $3.61/lb 4,229 107.0 4.80% Dollar peaks
Oct 27, 2023 ~$3.64 (+0.8%) 4,117 (-2.6%) 106.6 (-0.4%) 4.84% (+4bp) SPX low. 10Y already off its 5.02% high of the 23rd
Nov 15, 2023 ~$3.72 (+3.0%) 4,503 (+6.5%) 104.4 (-2.4%) 4.53% (-27bp) Yields break, SPX runs, copper trails
Dec 13, 2023 ~$3.85 (+6.6%) 4,707 (+11.3%) 102.9 (-3.8%) 4.02% (-78bp) Full rally underway
Jan 8, 2024 ~$3.79 (+5.0%) 4,764 (+12.6%) 102.4 (-4.3%) 4.03% (-77bp) Copper has given back ground; SPX has not

The sequence that actually ran:

  1. Oct 3 - Dollar peaks: DXY closes near 107 and starts rolling over
  2. Oct 23 - Yields fail: the 10-year tags 5.02% intraday and closes below 5%. It never trades there again. This is the single most important bar in the whole sequence, and it happened four days before SPX bottomed
  3. Oct 26-27 - Credit stops falling: HYG puts in its low for the year and turns up. SPX closes at 4,117 on the 27th
  4. Nov 1 - FOMC holds: the Fed leaves rates unchanged and is read dovish → dollar falls, bonds rally
  5. Nov 3 - Payrolls miss: the 10-year drops hard; SPX has its best week of the year
  6. Dec 13 - Fed dot plot: three cuts pencilled in for 2024; 10-year through 4.05%, SPX 4,707
  7. And copper? Roughly flat from 3 October through the SPX low, up about 6% by mid-December, and giving ground again in January while SPX kept going. On this move, copper was not the leader. Yields and credit were.

Read that last line carefully. "Copper leads equities by 1-3 months" is one of the most repeated claims in intermarket analysis and it did not hold here. The honest version of the framework is: several things lead, they take turns, and your job is to work out which one is doing the leading in this regime—not to memorise a hierarchy.

The Trade (If You Caught Intermarket Signals):

Action Date Price Rationale
BUY Signal Oct 27 SPX 4,117 10Y failed at 5%, DXY rolling over, HYG turning up
Add Position Nov 1 SPX 4,238 FOMC holds, yields breaking down, credit confirming
Take Profits Jan 8 SPX 4,764 Dollar stabilising, yields no longer falling, rally extended

Trade Results:

  • Entry avg: SPX 4,178 (blend of Oct 27 and Nov 1, equal size)
  • Exit: SPX 4,764
  • Gain: +586 points (+14.0%)
  • Holding period: 10 weeks
  • Risk: Stop at 4,050, below the 27 October intraday low of 4,104 = -128 points (-3.1%)
  • R:R realised: 586 / 128 = 4.6:1. Note that this is the realised ratio on a trade that worked, not an expected one—the same stop taken out would have been a 3.1% loss, and the framework gives you no way to know in advance which you are getting.

Why Intermarket Analysis Worked:

  1. Yields turned first: the 5% failure on 23 October preceded the SPX low by four sessions
  2. Credit confirmed: HYG bottomed on 26-27 October and never looked back—the cleanest single confirmation in the sequence
  3. Dollar reversal: DXY -4% over the window = a tailwind for commodities and EM
  4. The rate move did the heavy lifting: 10Y from 4.93% to 4.03% is a large repricing, and the long-duration end of the equity market took it best—QQQ ran roughly +18% over the same window against SPX +14%

Key Lesson: The advance warning here was days, not weeks, and it came from rates and credit rather than from copper. That is the realistic claim for intermarket analysis: it tells you which way the wind is blowing before the equity chart admits it, usually by a handful of sessions, and it tells you which leader to trust only after you have checked which one is actually leading.

Part 3: Intermarket Divergences (Advanced Signals)

💀 Sarah Chen's $42,800 Intermarket Blind Spot (3 Months)
August-October 2023: Sarah traded SPX purely on price action and RSI, going long every "oversold bounce." She ignored intermarket signals that screamed risk-off.
Example disaster (August 2023): She bought SPX at 4,420 after RSI hit 28 (oversold). Perfect setup on the daily chart.
What she missed: (1) 10Y Treasury yields spiked from 3.8% to 4.2% in two weeks (+40bps = major tightening), (2) DXY rallied 2.1% to 104.5 (dollar strength = risk-off), (3) Credit spreads (HYG/TLT) widening for 8th straight week (credit stress), (4) VIX at 18 and rising. All four intermarket indicators screamed "risk-off, liquidity draining, equities will fall."
She ignored them: "RSI is oversold, I'm buying." SPX closed 4,370 two days later and 4,200 by the end of September. Her stop hit at 4,360—1.36% below entry—on a $225K position: -$3,060. That is the shape of all 14 trades over the three months, and 14 × $3,060 is $42,800 of losses.
The fix: Before EVERY equity trade, check 3+ intermarket indicators (DXY, 10Y yields, HYG/TLT, VIX). If 3+ show risk-off, skip longs or reduce size 50%. What this buys you is not foresight—it is the discipline to notice when the only thing arguing for your trade is your own chart. Rule: equity technicals plus intermarket alignment = a trade. Equity technicals arguing with everything else = a coin flip you are paying commission on.

Divergence #1: Transports vs Industrials (Dow Theory)

Theory: For rally to be valid, transports (DJT) must confirm industrials (DJI)

Divergence setup: DJI making new highs, DJT lagging (not confirming)

Interpretation: Goods being produced (industrials) but not shipped (transports) = fake rally, inventory build-up

Trade: Bearish divergence → a reason to reduce equity exposure, not a signal on its own. Be honest about this one's record: DJT failed to confirm DJI for long stretches of 2015-16 and again in 2019 without the crash the theory implies, and the modern index is dominated by airlines and parcel carriers whose earnings track fuel prices and consumer shipping as much as industrial output. Treat it as one input, and check whether the divergence has a story behind it before you pay for puts.

Divergence #2: Small-Caps vs Large-Caps (IWM vs SPY)

Theory: Small-caps lead at cycle turns (more sensitive to economic conditions)

Bullish divergence: IWM bottoms and rallies while SPY still falling → recession ending, risk appetite returning

Bearish divergence: SPY making new highs but IWM failing to confirm → late cycle, institutions reducing risk

Divergence #3: Regional Equity Markets

Concept: Different regions lead/lag based on economic cycle

Leading Market Signal Implication for SPX
China (FXI, MCHI) Rallying while SPX flat Global growth reaccelerating (bullish)
Europe (EZU, VGK) Breaking down while SPX strong Regional stress (EUR crisis, energy shock)
Emerging Markets (EEM) Outperforming SPX Weak dollar, commodity rally (risk-on)

Part 4: Commodity Complex Analysis

The Commodity Curve: Backwardation vs Contango

Contango (normal): Future prices > spot prices (storage costs priced in)

Backwardation (demand surge): Spot prices > future prices (immediate shortage)

Trading implication:

  • Oil in backwardation → Supply shortage, demand strong → Bullish for energy stocks (XLE)
  • Oil in contango → the ordinary state of the curve, and on its own it says very little; a steepening contango while inventories build is the bearish version

Cross-Commodity Relationships

Gold vs Real Yields (Inverse Correlation)

Formula: Real Yield = 10Y Nominal Yield - Inflation Expectations

Relationship: Real yields UP → Gold DOWN (opportunity cost of holding gold increases)

Real yields DOWN → Gold UP (negative real yields = gold becomes attractive)

Trade: Falling real yields are a tailwind for gold, but the level is not a trigger—10-year real yields sat below -1% for most of 2021 and gold finished that year down. Treat real yields as one input alongside the dollar and central-bank buying, which is what carried gold through 2023-24 while real yields were firmly positive.

Copper/Gold Ratio (Growth Indicator)

Ratio rising: Copper outperforming gold → Growth accelerating (copper = industrial demand, gold = safe haven)

Ratio falling: Gold outperforming copper → Growth slowing, defensive rotation

Trade: Copper/gold ratio breaking out → buy cyclicals (XLI, XLB), sell defensives (XLP, XLU)

Part 5: Currency Market Insights

💀 Taylor Rodriguez's $28,400 Dollar-Blind Disaster (2 Months)
Over two months: Taylor traded equity breakouts on the 15-minute chart without ever opening a dollar chart. Every one of his setups was clean; the tape they were taken into was not.
Example disaster: He bought 400 NVDA at $125.60 on a 15-minute breakout—$50,240. Perfect technical setup.
What he missed: DXY had broken out and was up 2.8% in two weeks. A dollar rally of that speed drains liquidity from commodities, EM and the risk end of equities at the same time—it does not turn a good setup into a bad one, but it stacks the odds against every long in the book simultaneously, which is exactly what a run of 26 losers looks like.
NVDA reversed: the breakout failed and he was out at $122.80 three days later, -2.2% on the position: -$1,120. That pattern repeated 26 times in two months—26 losing trades averaging -$1,092. Total losses: $28,400.
The fix: Before your first equity trade each day, check DXY: (1) Above or below the 50 EMA? (2) Up or down over the past 5 days? (3) Moved 1.5% or more this week? A 1.5% weekly move in DXY is roughly a two-standard-deviation event—it happens a handful of times a year, so treat it as a rare hard stop rather than a daily filter. The everyday version is simpler: know which way the dollar has been going for the last week, and size accordingly.

EUR/USD as Risk Barometer

EUR/USD rising: Dollar weakness → Commodities rally, EM outperforms, SPX benefits (weaker USD = multinational earnings boost)

EUR/USD falling: Dollar strength → Commodities fall, EM underperforms, SPX headwinds

JPY as Safe Haven

USD/JPY falling (JPY strengthening): Risk-off, flight to safety → SPX likely declining

USD/JPY rising (JPY weakening): Risk-on → SPX likely rallying

Carry Trade Unwind Detection

Carry trade: Borrow in low-yield currency (JPY), invest in high-yield assets (equities, EM bonds)

Unwind signal: JPY suddenly strengthens + VIX spikes + EM currencies fall → Massive liquidation (2008, Aug 2015, March 2020)

Action: Exit all risk assets immediately (carry unwinds are violent and fast)

Quiz: Test Your Understanding

Q1: DXY breaks major support (dollar falling). What's your intermarket trade?

Show Answer

Answer: Long commodities (GLD, USO, COPX) and emerging markets (EEM). Weak dollar = commodities rally (priced in USD) + EM benefits (debt burden lightens). Consider rotating OUT of dollar-sensitive sectors into materials/energy.

Q2: SPY making new highs but HYG (high-yield bonds) falling. What's happening?

Show Answer

Answer: A divergence worth taking seriously, with one correction to the folklore: credit does not always lead equities—it led clearly in 2007 and in early 2020, and it lagged in 2018 and in 2022. And HYG is not a pure credit read: it carries real duration, so a falling HYG can be a rates story rather than a default story. Check what TLT is doing at the same time. If TLT is falling too, you are looking at rates; if TLT is flat or rising while HYG falls, that is genuine spread widening and it deserves the bearish reading—reduce equity exposure, and price puts before everyone else wants them.

Q3: Copper/gold ratio breaking out to new 6-month highs. What's the macro signal?

Show Answer

Answer: Growth reaccelerating. Copper (industrial demand) outperforming gold (safe haven) = risk-on, economic expansion. Buy cyclicals (XLI, XLB, XLE), rotate OUT of defensives (XLP, XLU). Small-caps (IWM) should also outperform.

Practical Checklist

Daily Intermarket Review (5 Minutes):

  • Check DXY: Breaking S/R? (impacts commodities, EM, multinational earnings)
  • Check 10Y yield: Rising fast (>20bps in week)? (pressures growth stocks)
  • Check HYG/SPY ratio: Diverging? (credit leading equities lower = warning)
  • Check copper: trend direction, and whether the move is demand or supply driven (the lead over SPX is real but unreliable)
  • Check VIX + USD/JPY: Both spiking? (carry trade unwind = sell everything)

Weekly Intermarket Deep Dive:

  • Calculate rolling 90-day correlation: SPX vs TLT, SPX vs DXY
  • Check for divergences: Transports vs Industrials, IWM vs SPY
  • Review commodity curves: backwardation (tight physical market) or a steepening contango against building inventories?
  • Use Signal Pilot Janus Atlas for multi-asset overlay (visual divergence check)
  • Track institutional flow via Pentarch Pilot Line (rotating into/out of risk assets?)

Key Takeaways

  • Causality chains: Dollar → Commodities → Inflation → Equities (understand the sequence)
  • Credit often leads equities: a HYG/TLT divergence is a reason to cut equity risk — check the ratio, not HYG alone, and remember credit lagged in 2018 and 2022
  • Copper/gold ratio = growth indicator (rising = risk-on, falling = defensive)
  • Correlations change by regime: SPX/TLT can be -0.6 (normal) or +0.3 (QE)
  • Carry trade unwind = sell everything: JPY spike + VIX spike = violent liquidation

Intermarket analysis will not make you predictive. What it does is stop you taking equity trades that every other market is arguing against—and occasionally, as in late October 2023, it puts you on the right side a few sessions before the equity chart admits anything. Watch the whole board rather than one piece, and hold the framework loosely: the leaders take turns, and the lags are nothing like as tidy as the diagrams suggest.
