Beyond Basic Sweeps: The Double Sweep Power Move

14 min read • Janus Atlas Mastery •

First sweep: $100 → $99.50 → $100.50. You go long. Stopped out.

Second sweep: Same level. $100 → $99.00 → $102.50.

You sit there, stopped out on the first, watching the second rally without you.

Why the count matters

A first sweep clears the stops that were sitting under the obvious level. A second sweep clears the stops placed by everyone who traded the first one. Those are two different pockets of liquidity, and the second is populated by people who thought they had waited.

So counting sweeps is what separates the bait from the entry — and the cost of waiting for the second one is priced later in this lesson, because waiting is not free.

Part 1: Advanced Sweep Patterns
📖 Deep dive: The 3 Questions to Ask Before Every Trade →

Pattern #1: The Double Sweep (the one that catches the most stops)

Setup: Price sweeps liquidity level twice before reversing. Institutions need more liquidity than single sweep provides.

🎯 Double Sweep Mechanics

Sequence:

  1. First sweep: $100 → $99.50 (clears obvious stops)
  2. Fake bounce: $99.50 → $100.80 (traps new longs)
  3. Second sweep: $100.80 → $98.80 (clears first sweep entries + new stops)
  4. REAL reversal: $98.80 → $103+ (institutions loaded, move begins)

Why it works: Second sweep collects 2-3× more liquidity than single sweep. Institutions need large size—second sweep provides it.

Identification:

  • Janus marks BOTH sweeps at same confluence zone
  • Second sweep typically 0.5-1× ATR deeper than first
  • Check Plutus: +8K delta or more on the second sweep means the liquidity was bought rather than the level simply giving way
  • Time between sweeps: 5-30 candles (5m-1H charts)

Execution:

  • Entry: After second sweep, wait for reclaim above first sweep level with volume
  • Stop: Below second sweep zone (NOT between sweeps)
  • Target: 2-4R to next structure level
  • What the filter buys you: requiring HTF trend agreement and order flow confirmation removes the sweeps that happen into an opposing trend, which is where this pattern fails. We are not going to quote a win rate for it

Pattern #2: Multi-Timeframe Sweep Confluence

Setup: HTF and LTF sweeps occur at same price level. A liquidity grab that clears both the 4H and the 15m stop clusters at once. How much size that represents is not visible from outside, and any figure put on it would be invented.

🎯 HTF/LTF Confluence Setup

Example:

  • 4H chart: Key support at $5,000 (session low, VWAP anchor)
  • 15m chart: LTF swing low at $4,998
  • Sweep zone: $4,995-4,997 (clears BOTH timeframes)

Why it's powerful: Institutions clear HTF stops (big size) + LTF stops (retail) = maximum liquidity at single price

Execution:

  • Entry: HTF reclaim with LTF volume spike (confirmation both TFs flipped)
  • Stop: Below sweep zone (example: $4,990)
  • Target: HTF target (example: $5,080 resistance) = 3-5R
Part 2: Execution Frameworks

Framework #1: Sweep-and-Reclaim Entry

🎯 The Reclaim Confirmation Rule

Core principle: Sweep alone is NOT a trade signal. Reclaim with volume = confirmation.

Conditions for entry:

  1. Janus sweep below support (example: $100 → $99.40)
  2. Price reclaims swept level (back above $100)
  3. Volume on reclaim candle > 1.5× average
  4. Plutus shows positive delta (+5K minimum)
  5. HTF trend supports direction (4H uptrend for longs)

Execution:

  • Entry: $100.20 (above reclaim, example)
  • Stop: $99.20 (below sweep)
  • Target: $103.50 (next resistance) = 3.3R
  • Time limit: If no reclaim within 5 candles, setup invalid

Why this one ranks highest: the reclaim is confirmation that has already happened. You are not predicting the reversal, you are waiting to be shown it, and you pay for that with a worse entry.

Framework #2: Failed Sweep Fade

🎯 When Sweeps Don't Reclaim

Setup: Sweep occurs but NO reclaim = weak buyers/sellers. Trade opposite direction.

Conditions:

  1. Sweep below $100 to $99.50
  2. NO reclaim within 5 candles
  3. Plutus shows continued selling (-3K+ delta)
  4. Price breaks down below the sweep low ($99.50)

Execution:

  • Entry: $99.20 (breakdown confirmed)
  • Stop: $100.50 (above failed sweep + structure)
  • Target: $96.50 (next support) = 2.1R

Where it stands: weaker than sweep-and-reclaim, because you are trading the absence of follow-through rather than the presence of a reclaim.

Framework #3: Failed Sweep Continuation

🎯 When Sweeps Don't Reverse

Conditions:

  1. Sweep below $100 to $99.50
  2. NO reclaim within 5 candles
  3. Continues to $98.50 (breakdown)
  4. Janus marks failed sweep
  5. HTF downtrend supports breakdown

Execution:

  • Example entry: $98.30 (breakdown confirmed)
  • Example stop: $99.80 (above failed sweep)
  • Potential target: $95.00 (next support) = 2.2R

Where it stands: the weakest of the three. You are betting that a failed sweep means the level is genuinely gone, and a level can be genuinely gone and still snap back.

Part 3: Combining Janus with Other Indicators
Janus + Plutus Flow (The Perfect Pair)

Sweep + Absorption: the liquidity was taken and somebody bought it

  • Janus sweep at $100
  • Plutus shows positive delta at sweep (+8,000)
  • Result: Strong buyer absorbed selling → Reversal UP indicated

Sweep + Exhaustion: Failed sweep likely

  • Janus sweep at $100
  • Plutus shows negative delta (selling dominates)
  • Result: No absorption → breakdown likely
Janus + Volume Oracle (Regime Alignment)

Sweep + Trending Regime: Trade pullback sweeps WITH trend

  • HTF trending UP
  • Janus sweep at support
  • Professional traders might consider entering long on reclaim

Sweep + Ranging Regime: Fade extremes

  • HTF ranging
  • Janus sweep at range low
  • Fade back to range mid/high
Janus + Harmonic Oscillator (Extreme Confirmation)

Sweep + Oversold (7/7 agreement): two unrelated methods pointing the same way

  • Harmonic shows 7/7 oversold agreement
  • Janus sweep at support
  • Result: the most independent agreement this toolkit can give you for a long

Sweep + Divergence: Additional confirmation

  • Price lower low, delta higher low (divergence)
  • Janus sweep at divergence level
  • Result: Triple confluence (sweep + divergence + harmonic)

⚠️ Common Janus Mistakes

Mistake #1: Trading Every Sweep

"Janus showed sweep, entering immediately!"

Fix: Require reclaim + volume + order flow confirmation. Not all sweeps reverse.

Mistake #2: Ignoring Failed Sweeps

Holding long after sweep failed to reclaim.

Fix: If no reclaim within 5 candles, exit. Consider reversal trade (short).

Mistake #3: Fighting HTF After Sweep

LTF sweep reversal long, but HTF downtrend.

Fix: Only trade sweeps aligned with HTF bias. Don't fight the trend.

📉 CASE STUDY: Jordan's $64,000 Single Sweep Trap (6 months)

Trader: Jordan Williams (composite example), 33, swing trader (2 years Janus Atlas experience, $108K account), Apr-Oct 2023

Strategy: Trading FIRST sweeps immediately after liquidity grabs. Jan-Mar 2023: 66% WR, +$14.8K using basic pattern (sweep → enter → reversal)

Fatal flaw: Only understood BASIC single sweep pattern. Never waited for SECOND sweep. In choppy markets, institutions use DOUBLE sweeps: first clears obvious stops, second clears early buyers, THEN real move. Jordan entered after first sweep every time, became fuel for second sweep

Result: 54 trades, 7 winners, lost $64K (-59%). He also kept a running note of the moves he was stopped out ahead of. Treat that column with suspicion — it assumes he would have held each of those positions all the way, which is exactly the assumption a stopped-out trader most wants to make. The $64K is what happened; the rest is what he tells himself happened.

The pattern (47 of 54 trades, 87%): First sweep → Jordan enters → Small bounce (10-30min confidence) → Second sweep stops him → Major move WITHOUT him. Examples: (1) Apr 3 NVDA: Support $270, first sweep $268.50, entered $269.20, stop $268. Bounced $271.40. Second sweep $267.20 stopped him. NVDA rocketed $278.60. Missed $9+ move, lost $600. (2) May 8 BTC: Support $27K, first sweep $26,750, entered $26,850, stop $26,600. Rallied $27,400. Second sweep $26,350 stopped him. BTC exploded $28,800. Missed $2K+ move, lost $1,250. (3) Jul 10 AMD: Support $110, first sweep $108.50 (50%), second sweep $107.80 (added 50%), THIRD sweep $106.50 stopped both. AMD rallied $116.80, lost $1,800. Breaking point (Aug 21): "47 of 54 followed the exact same pattern. I understood the basic version — sweep, then reversal — but not what happens in chop: first sweep, fake bounce, second sweep, then the real reversal. I was the liquidity, not the predator."

📈 Why his record should have worked, and did not

Both named equity and crypto examples carry an entry, a stop and a second sweep, so the geometry is measurable. Do it once for each.

            entry      stop     risk    2nd sweep   past his stop
NVDA      $269.20   $268.00    $1.20     $267.20         $0.80
BTC       $26,850   $26,600     $250     $26,350          $250

the move he named, in his own risk units
NVDA      $278.60 - $269.20  =  $9.40   /  $1.20   =  7.8R
BTC       $28,800 - $26,850  =  $1,950  /   $250   =  7.8R

break-even at 7.8 to 1    1 / (1 + 7.8)  =  11.3%
his hit rate                                 13%

Read those last two lines again, because they say something odd. On paper this record is marginally profitable. A 13% hit rate at 7.8 to 1 is above break-even. He lost 59% of the account.

The two facts are not in conflict, and the reason they are not is the entire lesson. The 7.8R is measured on a move he was not in for. An R-multiple calculated from a target you were swept out of before it printed is not a payoff — it is a description of the chart. His seven winners are the trades that tell you what he actually collected, and whatever it was, it was not 7.8R.

Now price the fix, because it is not free. A stop that survives the second sweep sits below it rather than above:

NVDA   stop $267.00   risk $2.20    $9.40 / $2.20  =  4.3R
BTC    stop $26,300   risk  $550    $1,950 / $550  =  3.5R

break-even at 4.3 to 1     1 / (1 + 4.3)  =  19.0%
break-even at 3.5 to 1     1 / (1 + 3.5)  =  22.0%

So the wider stop roughly doubles the distance, halves the position at constant dollar risk, and drops the payoff from 7.8 to about 4 — which raises the hit rate he needs from 11% to about 20%. That is the bar the double-sweep rule has to clear, and his record cannot tell you whether it does. Forty-seven of fifty-four losses sharing one failure mode says there is room. It does not say he gets there.

Recovery (Oct 2023-Mar 2024): New double sweep system: (1) Check regime first (trending = first sweep OK, ranging = wait for second). (2) Wait for second sweep in chop (0.5-1× ATR deeper than first). (3) Require HTF+LTF confluence (both timeframes showing liquidity at the same price). (4) Confirm +10K+ delta absorption on footprint at second sweep. (5) Stop BELOW second sweep zone (not between). (6) Trade far fewer setups. What changed is measurable in one number and one number only: he took 23 setups where he had taken 54, a 60% cut in entries. No win rate, profit or account figure is quoted for that stretch. Twenty-three trades cannot measure a filter, and the filter is the whole change — he is not reading the chart better, he is refusing more of it.

Jordan's lesson: "I lost $64K entering on FIRST sweeps and getting stopped by SECOND sweeps 47 times (87%). I understood basic Janus (sweep → reversal) but not ADVANCED pattern in choppy conditions: First sweep clears obvious stops → Fake bounce creates new stops → Second sweep grabs BOTH layers → REAL move. I was entering on fake bounce, BECOMING second sweep fuel. NVDA first $268.50 (I entered $269.20), second $267.20 stopped me, moved $278.60. BTC first $26,750 (I entered $26,850), second $26,350 stopped me, moved $28,800. 47 times. Now I wait for the second sweep in ranging conditions. HTF and LTF confluence. +10K delta absorption at the sweep. I take far fewer setups than I used to, and I have no idea yet whether the ones I take are better — what I do know is that I have stopped volunteering to be the fuel. In choppy markets the second sweep is the real entry. Being early is a way of being wrong."

Case Study Quiz: Jordan lost $64,000 (-59%) in 6 months with only 13% win rate trading Janus sweeps. His pattern (47 of 54 trades, 87%): First sweep → he enters → small bounce gives confidence → SECOND sweep stops him → major move without him. Examples: NVDA first sweep $268.50 (entered $269.20), bounced to $271.40 ("working!"), second sweep $267.20 stopped him, NVDA rocketed to $278.60 (missed $9+ move). BTC first sweep $26,750 (entered $26,850), rallied to $27,400, second sweep $26,350 stopped him, BTC exploded to $28,800 (missed $2K+ move). His previous success (66% WR, +$14.8K) came from trading basic single sweeps. What was Jordan's fatal mistake?

A) His stop losses were too tight (should widen stops beyond first sweep)
B) He only understood BASIC single sweep pattern. Never waited for SECOND sweep. In choppy markets, institutions use DOUBLE sweeps: first clears obvious stops, second clears early buyers who entered the bounce, THEN real move begins
C) He traded too frequently (54 trades in 6 months—should wait for higher probability setups)
D) He didn't check volume confirmation at sweep points (should require 3× avg volume)

Correct: B. Jordan's disaster: understanding only BASIC Janus (sweep → reversal) without recognizing ADVANCED double sweep in choppy markets. Basic works in trends: one sweep, reversal starts. In chop, institutions hunt liquidity in TWO layers. First sweep ($268.50) clears obvious retail stops. Creates bounce. Jordan enters thinking reversal started. But bounce creates NEW stops from early bulls. Second sweep ($267.20) grabs BOTH original stops AND new early-entry stops. Jordan becomes fuel for second sweep. THEN real reversal begins ($278.60), but he's stopped out. Destroyed him 47/54 trades (87%). BTC example: first sweep $26,750 → Jordan entered $26,850 → rallied $27,400 (confidence) → second sweep $26,350 stopped him → BTC exploded $28,800. Lost $1,250, missed $2K move. The $64K of losses is the part that actually happened; the 'missed profits' he tallied alongside it assume he would have held every one of those moves to the end, which no stopped-out trader can honestly claim. The fix: (1) check the regime — trending, the first sweep can be enough; ranging, wait for the second, (2) require the second sweep to be 0.5-1× ATR deeper, (3) require HTF and LTF confluence, (4) require +10K delta absorption at the sweep. Every one of those four subtracts entries rather than improving them, which is the point. Lesson: in chop the first sweep is bait and the second is where the liquidity is taken.

Test Your Understanding

🎮 Test Your Understanding (No Pressure)

First sweep: $100 → $99.50 → $100.50. Second sweep: $100 → $99.00 → $100.30. Footprint shows +10k delta at $99.00. What's the trade?

A) Long — double sweep + absorption, the most agreement available here
B) Skip — already failed once on first sweep
C) Short — support clearly broken
Why A is correct: this is the configuration with the most things agreeing at once. The double sweep cleared both layers of stops — the obvious ones and the early longs from the bounce — and +10k delta says someone bought that liquidity rather than the level simply failing. Example entry: $100.40, stop: $98.80, target: HTF resistance. No probability is attached: the stop below $98.80 is there because two sweeps and a large delta can still be followed by a third sweep.

Sweep to $99.50. No reclaim within 5 candles. Price continues to $98.50. What's the signal?

A) Wait for reclaim — it's just taking longer
B) Failed sweep — a short position might be considered on breakdown confirmation
C) Stay out — conflicting signals
Why B is correct: No reclaim within 5 candles = failed sweep. Support truly broken. A short at $98.30 with a stop at $99.80 and the next support as target is the trade the pattern describes. No probability is attached to it: as the framework itself says, this is the weakest of the three, because a level can be genuinely gone and still snap back.

You're watching ES futures. Daily chart shows support at $5,000 (session low). 15-min chart shows swing low at $5,002. Price sweeps to $4,997, then reclaims $5,005 with 2.5× volume and +12K delta on footprint. HTF shows uptrend. What's your trade?

A) Skip — price broke both support levels, downtrend confirmed
B) Wait for second sweep to confirm pattern
C) Long aggressively — multi-timeframe sweep confluence + strong reclaim + absorption + HTF trend, four things agreeing at once
Why C is correct: This is the perfect storm. Multi-timeframe confluence (Daily + 15m both swept at same zone) cleared massive liquidity. Reclaim above BOTH levels with 2.5× volume confirms reversal. +12K delta = institutional absorption. HTF uptrend supports long bias. No win rate is quoted for it, historically or otherwise — nobody has measured this setup across a sample worth the name, and a figure invented for a lesson would be worse than none. What can be said is that four independent things had to agree, which is as much as this method produces. Entry: $5,008, stop: $4,990 (below the sweep), target: next resistance $5,080+ = 4R minimum. Note where the stop sits: if $4,990 goes, all four readings were wrong together, which is exactly the risk you take when you stack confirmations.

The distinction that matters here is small and cheap to apply: count the sweeps before you decide the reversal has started. Being early to a real reversal and being wrong look identical for the first thirty minutes.

🎯 Practice: Classify Last Week’s Sweeps (Click to expand)

Three passes over charts you have already seen. Naming a pattern after the fact is the prerequisite for spotting it live:

  1. Mark last week’s sweeps on one chart — find every session where price took out a prior high or low and immediately reversed. Note whether it reclaimed the level, and how long that took.
  2. Classify five of them — double sweep, multi-timeframe confluence, failed-sweep fade, or failed-sweep continuation. Being able to name the pattern afterwards is the prerequisite for spotting it live.
  3. Write your reclaim rule down — decide in advance what counts as a reclaim (close back through the level? a set number of bars?) so you are not deciding it while the candle is forming.

🎓 Key Takeaways

  • The double sweep clears two layers of stops — the obvious ones, and then the ones placed by everyone who traded the first sweep. That is why it is worth waiting for, and it is not a probability
  • Confluence across timeframes marks the levels that matter — when daily and intraday sweep the same zone, the liquidity there was worth taking
  • Sweep-and-reclaim needs the reclaim — the sweep alone is not the entry, the recovery back through the level is
  • A failed sweep can fade or continue — which one depends on whether price accepts back inside the range or rejects it
  • An R-multiple you were stopped out of is not a payoff — Jordan's named trades were 7.8 to 1 measured from his own stop, which at a 13% hit rate is above break-even, and he still lost 59% of the account. The target was never available to him
  • A stop placed below the second sweep costs about half the payoff — 7.8 to 1 becomes roughly 4 to 1, so the hit rate it has to deliver rises from 11% to about 20%. Waiting is not free; it is a different trade
  • Wait for the liquidity event, then trade the reaction — not the anticipation of it
