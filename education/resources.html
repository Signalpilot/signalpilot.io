📥 Worksheets and Templates

Worksheets and templates that carry a lesson’s own measurement, with room for your number beside the one the lesson got.

Each one is an afternoon of arithmetic on your own record rather than a summary to read. You finish holding a number you did not have.

Lesson Worksheets

Each worksheet takes one lesson’s problems and gives them a shape to fill in. The right-hand column carries what the lesson measured on its own bars, so your answer has something to disagree with.

From Lesson 35: Sweeps, Beyond the First

Sweep Clearance Worksheet

Download

Count the levels your swing rule grants itself, sort every excursion into the three piles that two people arguing about the word are actually disagreeing over, then measure thirty of your own clearances and read off the hit rate a stop beyond three quarters of them would have to deliver.

Perfect for: Anyone who has called something a sweep without counting the levels first

💡 How to use these

  1. Work it, do not read it — a worksheet is worth nothing until its columns hold your own numbers
  2. Read the lesson first — the right-hand column only means something once you know where its number came from
  3. Keep your own numbers — a figure you measured beats a figure you were given, including every figure on these pages
  4. Redo them — lesson 74 measures how far a correlation moves between two halves of one record, and everything here has the same problem

⚠️ These Are Measurements, Not Shortcuts

None of these makes a method work. They make a method measurable, which is a smaller claim and the only one this course is willing to make.

If you have not read the lesson a worksheet names, it will produce numbers you cannot interpret. Read the lesson first; the worksheet is what you do afterwards.

Where each of these comes from

Every worksheet names the lesson it came from and every figure in its right-hand column is measured on that lesson’s own bars. Follow the link on the card to see the arithmetic.
