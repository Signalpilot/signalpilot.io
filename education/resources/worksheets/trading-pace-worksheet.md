# Trading Pace Worksheet

*Signal Pilot Education — companion to Lesson 76: The Pace You Actually Trade At.*

Lesson 65 turned a trade count into a wait by assuming forty trades a month, and said in the same breath that the forty was stated rather than derived. This worksheet derives yours. Everything downstream — how long a verdict takes, what a month earns, what a year of costs comes to — is that one number multiplied by something.

> **Educational only.** Trading involves substantial risk of loss. Not financial
> advice. Past performance does not guarantee future results. Every number in
> this worksheet is one you measure on your own record; the figures in the
> right-hand column are what the lesson measured on its own grid, and they are
> there to be disagreed with rather than copied.

## 1. Count orders, not opinions

Take a stretch of **at least twenty-eight moves** of the rule you actually run
and count position changes, not trade ideas. An entry is one, an exit is one.

call it a trade count.

## What you are holding when this is filled in

One pace and one wait. The pace is measured on your own orders rather than
assumed; the wait is what your record actually costs in months before it can
settle anything. Every other figure in the professional tier is one of these
two multiplied by something.
