# The rubric — written from the calibration pair, before touching a third lesson

Lesson 13 (best by the map, 2,735 words) and lesson 47 (worst, 6,439 words), both
read end to end in the brief's order: read → cut and reorder paragraphs → tune
voice at the seams → fix sentences → read again.

    L13   2735 -> 2707 words   cut 1  move 1  reword 3
    L47   6439 -> 6381 words   cut 2  move 1  reword 7

## What I cut

**A slot the lesson already has elsewhere, in a better version.** Lesson 47
carried the same six-instrument cross-market checklist four times: inside the
case study, inside the trader's closing quote, as the whole of Part 5, and again
in the practice exercise. Part 5 teaches it properly in steps, so that is the one
that survives. The case-study copy also restated a claim Part 1 had spent four
hundred words demolishing, which is what made it worse than redundant.

**A duplicated section heading.** Lesson 13 ended with a "Related Lessons" block,
and then a second "Related Lessons" block one screen later, with lesson 24 carded
in both under two different blurbs. The unique card from the second was moved into
the first and the second cut. Nothing in a per-section check sees this; the
heading is correct both times.

**Bullets that assert what the lesson disproves.** Lesson 47's case study said
"Stocks vs Bonds (inverse)" and "Dr. Copper never lies" — four bullets stating
the fixed-sign version that Part 1 exists to correct. Cut, not rewritten: the
fifth bullet and the arithmetic box beneath it are what the trader actually
learned, and they were already there.

## What I moved

**Lesson 47's Prerequisites sat above its standfirst.** A reader met a homework
list before learning what the lesson was about. Every other lesson opens on the
standfirst. Moved below it.

**Lesson 13's lesson-14 card**, lifted out of the duplicate block before that
block was cut, so no link was lost to a structural fix.

## What I reworded, and the one test

Only where a sentence is **false against the lesson's own spine**. That test did
all the work in lesson 47, and every hit was the same shape — the corrected
argument in the body, the uncorrected version in a box, a quote or a rule:

> Part 1: "The stock/bond correlation does not have a fixed sign... the practical
> question is never 'are they inversely correlated?'"
>
> The case study, four screens later: *"Bonds and stocks are INVERSE correlated."*

> Part 1: "Treat a copper breakdown as a reason to stop adding... **not as a date
> stamp.**"
>
> Three paragraphs below, in the same section: *"Trading rule: When copper rallies
> +5% in a week, add SPY longs."*

The replacement is always the conditional version, and it is the one place where
a replacement may be longer than what it replaced — a flat assertion is short
because it is wrong, and stating the condition costs words. Lesson 47's six
seam-level rewrites added 80 words against 1,500 characters cut.

## Register: what I tuned to

Lesson 14, the exemplar, and the two calibration lessons agree on it. Declarative.
One distinction per paragraph. Numbers where they exist and an explicit refusal
where they do not. Second person for what the reader does, third for what the
market does. No imperative that is not survivable if the trade fails — "stop
adding" survives, "add SPY longs" does not.

The failure mode to watch for in myself is the opposite of the corpus's: where the
old register overclaims, mine over-qualifies. Two of the six lesson 47 rewrites
came back longer than they needed to be, and I trimmed both before saving.

## What I left as written

- Lesson 47's five dated trades, each with a "what she missed" line. That looks
  like the repetition this pass cuts, and it is not: the yield climbing 4.10 →
  4.28 → 4.35 → 4.48 → 4.68 → 4.88 across the five is the argument, and the
  arithmetic box downstream needs the first one intact.
- Lesson 13's three opening boxes. The standfirst, "the uncomfortable part" and
  "why these rules exist" each do a different job. Only the premature 7% figure
  came out, because the table and the takeaways both give it in full.

## Batching from here

All 86 in groups of ten, lessons 1-9 included — they were read against a rubric
written from a different pass. Each batch opens on this file and lesson 14. Each
batch ends with one lesson read against lesson 14, and if the register has moved,
the batch is re-tuned before the next one starts.
