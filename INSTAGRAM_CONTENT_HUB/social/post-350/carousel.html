Slide 3 — Warning Signs
03 / 08

5 Warning
Signs

If any of these feel familiar, it is time to pause and reassess your approach before real damage is done.

1
No Written Plan
You enter trades without predefined entry, stop loss, and target levels. Every trade should be planned before you click the button, not after.
2
Emotional Entries
You feel excitement, revenge, or fear before entering. The urge to trade is coming from emotion, not from your system generating a valid signal.
3
Increasing Size After Losses
After a loss, you double down trying to recover quickly. This is the martingale trap and the fastest route to blowing an account.
4
Chasing Moves
You jump into positions after a big move because you are afraid of missing out. FOMO is a gambler's instinct, not a trader's edge.
5
No Proven Edge
You cannot articulate what your edge is. If you have never backtested your strategy or tracked your win rate, you are guessing, not trading.

Slide 5 — Self-Assessment
05 / 08

Honest
Self-Check

Answer honestly. No one sees this but you.

?
Do you plan every trade in writing before you execute it, with entry, stop, and target defined?
?
Can you walk away from the screen after placing a trade without checking it every few minutes?
?
Do you keep position size consistent regardless of whether your last trade was a win or a loss?
?
Can you describe your edge in one sentence, backed by data from at least 50 trades?
?
Do you review your journal weekly to identify patterns in your behavior and execution?
?
Are you comfortable sitting in cash for days when no valid setup appears?

If you answered "no" to three or more, your trading likely has gambling characteristics. That is not a judgment. It is an invitation to rebuild.

Slide 6 — Course Correction
06 / 08

Course
Correction

Recognizing the problem is step one. Here is how to rebuild your process from the ground up.

1
Stop Trading Immediately
Close all open positions. Step away for at least 48 hours. The market will still be there. Your account might not be if you continue in a gambling mindset.
2
Write Your Rules
Define your strategy on paper: entry criteria, exit criteria, position sizing, maximum daily loss. If it is not written, it does not exist.
3
Cut Size by 50%
When you return, trade at half your normal size. This removes the emotional weight and lets you focus on process over profit for at least 30 trades.
4
Journal Every Trade
Log the setup, your reasoning, your emotional state before entry, and the outcome. Patterns in your behavior will become undeniable within two weeks.
5
Set Hard Limits
Define a maximum daily loss and maximum number of trades per day. When either limit is hit, close the platform. No exceptions. No negotiations.

Slide 7 — Key Insight + Homework
07 / 08

The Core
Insight

"Trading is a business. Gambling is entertainment. The moment your account becomes a source of excitement rather than income, you have crossed the line."

The Trader's Mindset
⚙
Process Over Outcome A losing trade taken with discipline is a good trade. A winning trade taken on impulse is still a bad trade.
⚠
Awareness Is Defense You cannot fix what you do not notice. Self-awareness is the first line of defense against tilt.
Homework

Review your last 20 trades. For each trade, write down whether you had a written plan before entry. Calculate the win rate and average R:R for planned trades vs unplanned trades. The difference will tell you everything you need to know about where you stand.

Remember

Every professional trader has crossed this line at some point. The difference between amateurs and professionals is that professionals recognize it faster and correct sooner. There is no shame in the slip. Only in refusing to acknowledge it.
