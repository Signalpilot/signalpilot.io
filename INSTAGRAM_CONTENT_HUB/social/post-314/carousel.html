Ego Trading vs. Edge Trading

Ego Trader
"I know where this is going"
Moves stop loss to avoid being wrong
Adds to losing positions
Takes losses personally
Measures success by being right
Edge Trader
"I know my plan, the market decides"
Honors stop loss every time
Cuts losers fast, no exceptions
Takes losses as data
Measures success by process

SCENARIOS

React, Don't Predict

WRONG "BTC will hit $80k this week — I'm going all in long." You're guessing, not trading. When it drops, you'll freeze.
RIGHT "If BTC breaks above this resistance with volume, I'll enter long with a stop below the breakout candle." You have a plan for what IS, not what you think should be.
WRONG "This support has held 5 times, it can't break." It can. It will. And your stubbornness will be the liquidity it uses to break through.
RIGHT "If support holds, I enter. If it breaks, I step aside or look for the retest short." You've covered both outcomes before they happen.

MINDSET SHIFTS

4 Rules for Acceptance

01
Anything Can Happen
Every trade has an uncertain outcome. Accept this before you enter, not after you lose.
02
The Next Trade Is Random
Your edge plays out over 100 trades, not 1. Each individual trade is essentially a coin flip within your edge.
03
Being Wrong Is Normal
The best traders are wrong 40-50% of the time. They profit because of R:R, not win rate.
04
Process Over Outcome
A losing trade executed correctly is a win. A winning trade that broke your rules is a loss.
The Shift
Your job isn't to be right — it's to manage risk when you're wrong. This is the only thing that separates profitable traders from everyone else.
Tonight's Homework
Write Your If/Then Plan
Before tomorrow's session, write an if/then plan covering 3 scenarios. If price does X, I do Y. If it does Z, I do nothing. When the market moves, you'll respond instead of react. This single habit separates professionals from gamblers.
