Timeline

The Three Phases

PHASE 1
Paper
4–8 Weeks
Learn your system. Build mechanical execution. Get 50+ trades logged. Track win rate & R:R.
PHASE 2
Micro
4–8 Weeks
Smallest position size possible. Real money, minimal risk. Feel the emotions. Same rules.
PHASE 3
Live
Gradual Scale
Increase size by 25% every 2 weeks if metrics hold. If they slip, drop back one level.
Key Insight
Paper trading teaches you the system. Live trading teaches you yourself. You can’t learn the second without starting the first.

Assessment

Are You Ready to Go Live?

Ready If
✔ 50+ paper trades logged
✔ Positive expectancy over 4+ weeks
✔ Written rules you follow consistently
✔ You can explain your edge clearly
✔ Risk management is automatic
Not Ready If
✗ Still changing system weekly
✗ No trade journal or logs
✗ Can’t define your entry rules
✗ Position sizing is random
✗ Emotional decisions dominate

Readiness isn’t about perfection. It’s about process.

05 / 08
Transition

Your Paper-to-Live Plan

1
Pick your smallest position
Whatever your broker’s minimum is. The goal is experiencing real money emotions, not making profit.
2
Keep the same rules
Exact same system. Same entries, same exits, same risk per trade. Only the capital is real.
3
Journal every emotion
Write down what you felt before, during, and after each trade. This is the data paper couldn’t give you.
4
Scale only with proof
2 profitable weeks at current size before increasing. If metrics drop, go back down. No ego.

06 / 08
Mistakes

Pitfalls in the Transition

01
Going Full Size Immediately
Paper to full-size is emotional whiplash. The jump should feel almost boring. If it’s exciting, your size is too big.
02
Changing the System
“It worked on paper, but I’ll just tweak this one thing.” Now you’re trading a new, untested system.
03
Ignoring the Journal
Emotions are the new variable. Without journaling, you have no data on the most important factor — you.
04
Revenge After First Loss
Your first real loss will test everything. Plan for it. Expect it. Have a protocol: walk away for 30 minutes.

Why We Stay Too Long

The Comfort Trap
Paper trading feels productive. You’re “practicing.” But after a point, you’re practising the absence of risk — which is the one thing you need to learn to handle.
