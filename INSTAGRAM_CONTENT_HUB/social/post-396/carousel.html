Slide 4 — Execution Quality
04 / 08

What to Track:
Execution Quality

A winning trade with poor execution is a problem waiting to repeat. Grade yourself on how you traded, not just the result.

✓
Did You Follow Your Rules?
Entry criteria met? Position size within risk limits? Stop loss placed before entry? Rate your rule adherence on a simple Yes / Partial / No scale.
✓
Entry Timing Quality
Did you enter at the planned level or chase? Was there slippage? Could you have waited for a better fill? Grade A through D.
✓
Exit Timing Quality
Did you exit at target or panic-close? Did you hold too long out of greed? Was the exit decision-based or emotional? Grade A through D.
✓
Overall Execution Score
Combine rule adherence, entry, and exit grades into a single score. A well-executed loss is better than a lucky, undisciplined win.

“Separate the quality of the decision from the quality of the outcome. Great process with bad luck is still great trading.”

Slide 5 — Emotional State
05 / 08

What to Track:
Emotional State

Your emotional state is the invisible variable that sabotages good strategies. Tracking it turns subconscious patterns into conscious awareness.

1
Before the Trade
How did you feel before entering? Calm and focused? Anxious to recover a loss? Overconfident from a winning streak? Name the emotion in one word.
2
During the Trade
Did you feel the urge to move your stop? Did you check price every 30 seconds? Were you tempted to add size? Track the internal dialogue.
3
After the Trade
Win or lose, what was your immediate reaction? Relief? Frustration? Euphoria? Did the result change your plan for the next trade?
Emotion Log Example
Before Focused — clear plan, no revenge trading
During Anxious — tempted to close at +15 pips
After Satisfied — held to plan, hit TP1

Slide 6 — Lessons Learned
06 / 08

What to Track:
Lessons Learned

Every trade teaches something. The lesson section transforms raw experience into actionable insights that compound over weeks and months.

▲
Strength
What Went Well

Identify the decisions that worked. Was your read correct? Did patience pay off? Reinforcing good behavior is just as important as fixing mistakes.

◆
Growth
What Could Improve

Be specific. Not just “I exited too early” but “I closed at +20 pips because of fear, missing the +55 pip move to TP2.”

●
Action
Actionable Takeaway

Write one sentence you can act on. Example: “Next time at +1R, take 25% and set a trailing stop instead of closing the full position.”

▬
Pattern
Recurring Theme

After 20+ entries, tag recurring lessons. If “exited too early” appears in 40% of your trades, that is a systemic issue worth a dedicated fix.

Slide 7 — Weekly Review
07 / 08

Review
Weekly

Individual entries are raw material. The weekly review is where you mine them for gold. Set aside 30 minutes every weekend to find the signal in the noise.

1
Spot Patterns in Your Data
Which setups have the best win rate? Which sessions are most profitable? Do you trade better on certain days? Let the numbers speak before your ego does.
2
Identify Recurring Mistakes
Look for the same mistake appearing three or more times. That is not bad luck — it is a habit. Write it on a sticky note and put it on your monitor.
3
Double Down on What Works
If your London session breakout trades have a 68% win rate while your late-NY scalps lose money, the journal just wrote your next trading plan for you.
4
Set One Goal for Next Week
Pick a single, measurable improvement target. “Follow my stop-loss rules on 100% of trades” beats vague resolutions like “trade better.”
Homework

Create your journal template today. Use a spreadsheet, Notion page, or even a physical notebook. Include columns for date, symbol, entry/exit, P&L, setup type, confidence, execution grade, emotional state, and one lesson learned. Log your next 10 trades using this template, then do your first weekly review. The patterns will surprise you.
