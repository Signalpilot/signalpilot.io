Slide 2 — Definition

What Is Correlation?

Correlation Coefficient
A value from -1.0 to +1.0 that measures how closely two assets move in relation to each other over a given period.
-1.0 0 +1.0
Why It Matters
Holding two long positions in highly correlated assets is really one big trade — your risk is double what you think.

Slide 3 — Types

Three Types

+1
Positive
Assets move in the same direction at the same time
EUR/USD & GBP/USD
0
None
No predictable relationship — movements are independent
Gold & Soybeans
-1
Negative
Assets move in opposite directions — natural hedge
USD/JPY & Gold
Key Insight
Correlation is not constant — it shifts with market regimes, volatility, and macro events. Always check current values.

Slide 4 — Rules

Four Correlation Rules

01
Check Before You Stack
Never open two positions in highly correlated pairs without reducing size on both
02
Hedge With Inverse
Use negative correlation for hedging — long one, short the inverse for reduced drawdown
03
Monitor Regime Shifts
Correlations break during crisis events — what normally hedges may suddenly move together
04
Diversify Genuinely
True diversification requires uncorrelated assets — not just different ticker symbols

Key Truth

Diversification is only real when your assets are genuinely uncorrelated. Everything else is an illusion of safety.
