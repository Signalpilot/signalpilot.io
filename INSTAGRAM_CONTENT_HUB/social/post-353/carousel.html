Slide 4 — Trailing Stops
04 / 08

Trailing Stops
Deep Dive

A trailing stop follows price as it moves in your favor. The key is choosing the right method for the current market conditions.

1
Fixed Percentage Trail
Trail your stop a fixed percentage below the highest point. Works best in strong, steady trends. Example: 2% trail on a swing trade.
2
ATR-Based Trail
Use 1.5x to 2x ATR below the recent high. This adapts to volatility automatically. Wider in volatile markets, tighter in calm ones.
3
Structure-Based Trail
Move your stop below each higher swing low. This respects market structure and only exits when the trend actually breaks.
4
Moving Average Trail
Use the 20 EMA or 50 SMA as a dynamic trailing stop. Exit when price closes below the moving average on your trading timeframe.

“The best trailing stop is the one that keeps you in winners long enough while protecting your gains. There is no single right answer.”

Partial Profits
Strategy

Taking partial profits is a balancing act. Secure gains too early and you leave money on the table. Too late and you give it all back.

Take 25-33% off, move stop to breakeven

Price hits major resistance

Momentum fades sharply

Close remaining position at market

Slide 6 — Moving to Breakeven
06 / 08

Moving to
Breakeven

The breakeven stop eliminates risk on a trade. But moving too early can get you stopped out of a winner. Timing is everything.

1
Wait for Confirmation
Do not move to breakeven the moment price moves in your favor. Wait until the trade has proven itself with at least a 1:1 R:R move or clear structure shift.
2
Add a Buffer
Instead of moving exactly to entry, add a small buffer (a few pips or points above entry for longs). Markets often retest entry zones before continuing.
3
Match the Timeframe
Scalpers can move to breakeven quickly. Swing traders need more room. A 4H trade should not be managed with 5-minute decisions.
4
Do Not Force It
Sometimes the trade needs room to breathe. If your thesis is still valid and structure is intact, premature breakeven stops kill good trades.

“Breakeven is not about protecting your P&L. It is about giving yourself a risk-free shot at a bigger move.”

Live Trade
Scenario

EUR/USD Long — 4H Swing
1
Entry: Buy at 1.0850 on a breakout above the descending trendline. Stop loss at 1.0810 (40 pip risk).
2
Price hits 1.0890 (+40 pips, 1:1 R:R). Take 25% profit. Move stop to 1.0855 (just above entry).
3
Price hits 1.0930 (+80 pips, 2:1 R:R). Take another 25% profit. Trail stop to 1.0880 (below last swing low).
4
Price reaches 1.0960 but momentum fades. Close remaining 50% as bearish divergence forms on RSI.
5
Result: Average exit around +70 pips with risk eliminated after step 2. No stress, no regret, disciplined execution.
Homework

Open your journal and review your last 10 trades. For each one, write down what management decisions you made after entry. Were there moments where trailing your stop or taking partial profits would have improved the outcome? Create a management checklist you can follow on every future trade.
