Wrong Reasons
to Size Up

These are emotional triggers disguised as logic. If any of these sound familiar, your sizing is driven by feelings, not data.

⚠
Revenge
“I Need to Make Back Losses”

Sizing up after a drawdown is the fastest way to compound your losses. Recovery requires patience, not aggression.

★
Overconfidence
“This Setup Is Perfect”

No setup has a 100% win rate. The moment you believe a trade cannot fail, you have already lost objectivity.

☘
Emotion
“I Feel Lucky Today”

Luck is not a strategy. Position sizing should never change based on mood, hunches, or gut feelings.

⏱
Recency
One Good Week

A single profitable week is noise, not signal. You need months of consistency before adjusting your risk upward.

Critical Warning

Sizing up for the wrong reasons is the number one account killer among developing traders. It turns small drawdowns into catastrophic ones.

Slide 3 — Right Reasons
03 / 07

Right Reasons
to Size Up

Size increases should be earned through evidence. Every one of these criteria must be met before you consider increasing risk.

✓
Months of Consistent Profitability
Not one good week or one lucky month. You need a track record of at least 3-6 months of positive expectancy before even considering a size increase.
✓
Data-Backed Confidence
Your journal shows a proven edge: positive win rate, healthy R:R, and manageable drawdowns. Numbers do not lie. Use them to justify the decision.
✓
Account Has Grown
Your account balance has increased organically through consistent execution. The larger account naturally supports a slightly bigger position without changing your risk percentage.
✓
Risk Tolerance Confirmed
You have experienced a full drawdown cycle at your current size and handled it without breaking rules. Emotional stability at the current level is required before leveling up.

How to Increase
Gradually, Not Suddenly

Position size should scale like a staircase, not an elevator. Small increments give your psychology time to adjust to the new dollar amounts at risk.

1.00%
→
Starting base risk. Proven over months of consistent execution. This is your anchor.
1.25%
→
First increment. After 4+ weeks profitable at 1%. Watch for emotional changes.
1.50%
→
Second increment. Only if drawdown stayed within tolerance at 1.25%. No rule violations.
1.75%
→
Third increment. You should barely notice the difference at this point. That is the goal.
2.00%
→
Target risk. Earned through discipline and evidence over multiple months of stepping up.
Never Do This

Jumping from 1% to 5% in a single move. This is not scaling. This is gambling. Your psychology cannot absorb a 5x increase in dollar risk overnight.

“If your new position size makes you anxious watching the screen, you have increased too fast. Scale back immediately.”

Slide 5 — When to Decrease
05 / 07

When to
Decrease Size

Knowing when to step back is just as important as knowing when to step up. These are non-negotiable triggers for reducing your position size.

1
Hit Your Drawdown Threshold
If your account drops beyond your predefined maximum drawdown (e.g., 5-10% from equity high), reduce size by 50% immediately. This is not optional. It is survival.
2
Losing Streak Exceeds Norm
If your consecutive losses exceed what your backtesting predicted (e.g., 6+ losses in a row when your model expects a max of 4), step down. The edge may have shifted.
3
Emotional Instability
If you notice yourself breaking rules, revenge trading, moving stops, or feeling anxious about open positions, your size is too large for your current state.
4
Changing Market Conditions
When volatility spikes, correlations break, or your strategy stops performing, reduce exposure first and investigate second. Capital preservation trumps everything.

Slide 6 — The Sizing Test
06 / 07

The Sizing
Test

Before placing any trade, run this simple mental check. If you cannot answer honestly, you are not ready for your current size.

Ask Yourself
“If this trade loses, will I still follow my rules on the very next trade?”
✓
Yes

Your size is appropriate. The loss is within your psychological tolerance. Proceed with the trade as planned.

✗
No

Your size is too big. The potential loss will trigger emotional decisions. Reduce size until the answer becomes yes.

1
Dollar Amount Check
Calculate the exact dollar loss if stopped out. Say it out loud. If it makes you uncomfortable, that is your answer.
2
Sleep Test
Can you leave this position open overnight without checking your phone? If not, you are sized too heavy for your risk tolerance.
3
Three-Loss Scenario
Imagine losing this trade and the next two in a row at this size. Would you still be calm? Would you still follow your plan? Size accordingly.

“The right position size is the one that lets you think clearly, act rationally, and execute your plan without hesitation.”
