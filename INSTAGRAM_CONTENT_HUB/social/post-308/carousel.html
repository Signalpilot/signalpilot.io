Definition

What Is a Breaker Block?

Core Concept
A breaker block is a failed order block that flips into a zone of the opposite bias. When price breaks through an order block instead of respecting it, that zone becomes a breaker — a high-probability reversal area on the retest.
OB
Order Block
→
Fails
BRK
Breaker Block

Think of it as support becoming resistance — but with institutional footprints as evidence.

Formation

How Breaker Blocks Form

1
Order Block Establishes
Price creates an order block — a zone of institutional buying or selling. Traders expect this zone to hold.
2
Price Breaks Through
Instead of respecting the OB, price slices through it with momentum. The order block has failed. Stops are triggered.
3
Breaker Block Forms
The failed OB zone flips. What was support becomes resistance (or vice versa). This is the breaker block — a high-probability retest zone.
Why It Works
Trapped traders from the original OB now need to exit. Their exits create the liquidity that fuels the reversal at the breaker level.

Types

Two Types of Breakers

Bullish Breaker
Support Flip
✔ Bearish OB forms (selling zone)
✔ Price breaks above the OB
✔ Failed sellers become trapped
✔ Retest of zone = buying opportunity
✔ Old resistance becomes new support
Bearish Breaker
Resistance Flip
✔ Bullish OB forms (buying zone)
✔ Price breaks below the OB
✔ Failed buyers become trapped
✔ Retest of zone = selling opportunity
✔ Old support becomes new resistance

The key: breakers trade the failure of the original thesis, not the thesis itself.

Identification

How to Spot a Breaker

01
Find the Order Block
Identify a clear OB where price previously reacted. The last up-candle before a drop, or last down-candle before a rally.
02
Confirm the Break
Price must close through the OB body — not just wick through it. A decisive break with strong candle close.
03
Mark the Zone
The body of the original OB candle is now your breaker zone. Draw it from the open to close of that candle.
04
Wait for the Retest
Price should return to the breaker zone. This retest is your trade opportunity — not the break itself.
Pro Tip
The strongest breakers form on higher timeframes (4H, Daily) and align with a change of character (CHoCH) in market structure. If the breaker also coincides with a fair value gap, the probability increases.

Execution

Trading the Breaker Block

ENTRY
Enter on the retest of the breaker zone. Look for a reaction candle (rejection wick, engulfing) for confirmation.
STOP
Place stop beyond the breaker zone — below for bullish breakers, above for bearish. If the breaker fails too, the thesis is invalid.
TARGET
Aim for the next liquidity pool or opposing order block. Minimum 2:1 R:R. Let the trapped traders fuel your move.
  • ✔ Always wait for the retest — don’t chase the break
  • ✔ Higher timeframe breakers have higher probability
  • ✔ Combine with CHoCH and fair value gaps for confluence
  • ✔ Volume confirmation strengthens the setup
  • ✔ First retest is the highest probability — second is weaker

Mistakes

What Traders Get Wrong

01
Trading the Break
Entering on the break itself instead of the retest. The break confirms the zone; the retest is the trade.
02
Ignoring Timeframe
A breaker on the 1-minute chart has far less significance than one on the 4-hour. Context matters.
03
No Confluence
Using breakers in isolation without checking structure, trend, or liquidity targets. Breakers work best with supporting evidence.
04
Stale Breakers
Breakers lose potency after multiple retests. The first retest is the highest probability. After 2–3 touches, move on.

The concept is simple. The discipline to wait for the retest is the hard part.
