THE CONCEPT
All Positions Closed
Before the Bell
D
DAY TRADING
Opening and closing all trades within the same session. No overnight holds. Uses 1-minute to 15-minute charts. Requires full, undivided attention during market hours.
T
TIMEFRAMES
5-minute and 15-minute for entries. 1-hour and 4-hour for context and daily bias. Trades last minutes to hours. Fast decisions, fast feedback, fast consequences.
!
REQUIREMENTS
Full attention during market hours. Fast decisions under pressure. Strict discipline, reliable internet, adequate capital ($25K+ for US PDT rules), and a tested, proven strategy.

THE ROUTINE
A Day Trader's
5-Step Session
6:30 AM — PRE-MARKET
Prep & Plan
Review overnight price action, mark key levels, check economic calendar, define your bias. Never trade without a plan made with a clear mind.
9:30 AM — OPENING RANGE
Observe First 30 Min
Let the market reveal its hand. The first 30 minutes are for watching, not trading. Define the opening range high and low.
10:00 AM — EXECUTION
Execute Your Setups
When pre-defined setups appear at pre-marked levels, execute without hesitation. The plan was made with clarity. Trust it now.
12:00 PM — MID-DAY CHECK
Assess & Protect
Check P&L and emotional state. If you're up, protect gains. If you're down, don't revenge trade. Consider stopping early.
3:45 PM — CLOSE
Flatten & Journal
Close all positions before the bell. Journal every trade: what worked, what didn't, what you'll improve tomorrow. The journal is the edge.

The 6 Skills
Every Day Trader Needs
R
Reading Price
Understanding what each candle means in context. No indicator can replace the ability to read raw price action.
FOUNDATION
L
Level ID
Day trading is a levels game. Support, resistance, VWAP, previous day's high/low. Trades happen at levels.
ESSENTIAL
D
Discipline
Sticking to the plan when adrenaline says otherwise. The best day traders are the most boring ones.
NON-NEGOTIABLE
S
Speed
Entries and exits must be precise. Pre-set orders, use hotkeys. Milliseconds matter when the market moves fast.
MECHANICAL

Emotional Ctrl

PSYCHOLOGY

COMMON TRAPS
Three Mistakes That
Destroy New Day Traders
1
OVERTRADING
Taking 20+ trades a day because you're "in the zone." More trades does not mean more profit. Quality over quantity. 3–5 well-planned trades per session is enough for most strategies.
2
REVENGE TRADING
After a loss, doubling size to "get it back." This is how one bad trade becomes five. Walk away from the screen. The market will be there tomorrow. Your capital might not be.
3
SKIPPING THE JOURNAL
If you don't review your trades, you cannot improve systematically. The journal is where patterns emerge, mistakes get caught, and your real edge gets refined over hundreds of trades.
THE RULE OF THREE
Three consecutive losses = stop trading for the day. No exceptions. This single rule prevents more blown accounts than any indicator or strategy ever could.

DAY VS SWING
Which Style Fits
Your Life?
⏰
Time Available
Day trading needs 4–6 hours of focused screen time daily. Swing needs 30 minutes. Be brutally honest about your schedule and commitments.
🧠
Personality Type
Day traders thrive on intensity and fast decisions. Swing traders prefer patience and analysis. Neither is inherently better — just different temperaments.
💰
Capital Needed
US PDT rules require $25K minimum for day trading. Swing trading has no such restriction. Start where your capital allows, not where your ego wants.
📈
Learning Curve
Day trading has a steeper curve because mistakes happen faster and more frequently. Swing trading gives you more time to think, analyze, and learn.

"The best trading style is the one you can execute consistently without burning out."
