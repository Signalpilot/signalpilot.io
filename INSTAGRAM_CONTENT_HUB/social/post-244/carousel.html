Slide 2 — The Problem
02 / 08
THE TRAP

Outcome Bias
Destroys Traders

A bad trade that makes money teaches you the wrong lesson. A good trade that loses teaches you nothing — if you let the result define it.
Outcome Thinking
Process Thinking
× “I made money, so it was a good trade”
✓ “Did I follow my rules?”
× “I lost, so I need to change my system”
✓ “Was my thesis valid at entry?”
× “My win rate dropped — panic”
✓ “Am I executing consistently?”
DANGER
Outcome bias makes you repeat bad habits that happened to win, and abandon good habits that happened to lose. Over time, this is fatal.

Slide 4 — The Paradox
04 / 08
THE PARADOX

The Worst
Win

The most destructive thing in trading isn’t a loss. It’s a win on a bad trade. Because it teaches you that breaking rules works.
A
Best trade of the week
LOST -1R
Perfect setup. Valid thesis. Clean entry. Stop hit by 2 ticks. Would take this trade 100 more times. The edge plays out over the sample.
F
Worst trade of the week
WON +4R
No setup. No plan. Entered on tilt. Happened to catch a trend day. This win is debt you’ll pay back with interest.
TRUTH
Your best trade and your most profitable trade are almost never the same trade. Learn to separate the two.

Slide 6 — The Shift
06 / 08
DAILY DRILL

The Process
Ritual

01
Pre-trade: Write your thesis
Before entering, write down: setup type, entry reason, stop level, target, and risk amount. If you can’t articulate it, don’t take it.
02
During: Follow the plan
No improvising. No moving stops. No “just this once.” The plan is the plan. Trust the process you designed when you were thinking clearly.
03
Post-trade: Grade before checking P&L
Score your execution across all six dimensions. Write notes. Then look at the result. The grade matters more than the dollars.
04
Weekly: Review the grades, not the equity
Plot your average execution grade over time. If the grade is improving, the equity will follow. If the equity rises but grades drop — trouble is coming.
