Slide 3 — Continuation Patterns
03 / 08

Continuation
Patterns

These form within a trend and signal that the move is likely to continue after a brief pause or consolidation.

▶
Continuation
Bull Flag

A sharp rally followed by a tight, downward-sloping channel. The breakout continues the prior trend upward.

▼
Continuation
Bear Flag

A sharp decline followed by a slight upward drift. Breakdown resumes the prior downtrend.

◢
Continuation
Pennant

Converging trendlines after a strong move. Volume contracts, then price breaks in the direction of the prior leg.

△
Continuation
Ascending Triangle

Flat resistance with rising lows. Buyers are becoming more aggressive. Breakout is typically upward.

▽
Continuation
Descending Triangle

Flat support with lower highs. Sellers gain control. Breakdown is the expected resolution.

◊
Either Direction
Symmetrical Triangle

Converging trendlines with no bias. Breaks in the direction of the prior trend more often than not.

Slide 4 — Reversal Patterns
04 / 08

Reversal
Patterns

These form at the end of a trend and signal that the dominant move is losing momentum and may reverse.

⌀
Reversal
Double Top

Price tests the same resistance twice and fails. The neckline break confirms the bearish reversal.

⌆
Reversal
Double Bottom

Price tests the same support twice and bounces. The neckline break confirms a bullish reversal.

▲
Reversal
Head & Shoulders

Three peaks with the middle highest. Breaking the neckline signals a bearish trend change.

▼
Reversal
Inv. Head & Shoulders

Three troughs with the middle deepest. A neckline break confirms bullish reversal of a downtrend.

↗
Reversal
Rising Wedge

Converging trendlines sloping upward. Momentum fades. Breakdown is bearish despite the upward slope.

↘
Reversal
Falling Wedge

Converging trendlines sloping downward. Selling pressure weakens. Breakout to the upside is bullish.

Slide 5 — Pattern Anatomy
05 / 08

Pattern
Anatomy

Every pattern has three key elements. Know them before you trade.

Trade Structure
E
Entry Point
Enter on the breakout or breakdown of the pattern boundary. Wait for confirmation, typically a candle close beyond the level, not just a wick.
S
Stop Loss
Place your stop on the other side of the pattern. For breakouts, below the last swing low inside the formation. For breakdowns, above the last swing high.
T
Measured Target
Measure the height of the pattern at its widest point. Project that distance from the breakout level. This is your minimum expected move.
Pro Tip

Always calculate your risk-to-reward before entering. If the measured target does not give you at least 1:1.5 R:R, the setup may not be worth taking.

Volume
Confirmation

A pattern without volume is a suggestion. A pattern with volume is conviction.

Volume Profile
Low Vol
✗
Average
≈
High Vol
✓
Spike
✓✓
▲
High Confidence

Breakout on above-average volume with a strong candle close beyond the pattern.

▼
Low Confidence

Breakout on declining or below-average volume. Likely to fail or retrace quickly.

Key Rule

Volume should expand on the breakout and contract during consolidation within the pattern. If volume is flat or declining at breakout, wait for a retest before committing.

Slide 7 — Checklist + Homework
07 / 08

Identification
Checklist

✓
Identify the Trend Context
Is this pattern forming in an uptrend, downtrend, or range? Context determines whether you are looking at continuation or reversal.
✓
Draw the Boundaries
Connect at least two swing highs and two swing lows to define the pattern. The more touches, the stronger the pattern.
ⓘ
Check Volume Behavior
Volume should contract as the pattern develops and expand on the breakout. Divergence here is a warning sign.
!
Wait for Confirmation
A candle close beyond the boundary is your signal. Do not enter on the first wick. Patience prevents false breakout losses.
✓
Align with Higher Timeframe
A pattern on the 1H chart is more reliable when the 4H and Daily trends agree. Multi-timeframe confluence is key.
Homework

Open your charting platform and find 3 active chart patterns across different timeframes. For each one, identify the type (continuation or reversal), draw the boundaries, and mark where your entry, stop, and target would be. Save screenshots and review them in 48 hours.
