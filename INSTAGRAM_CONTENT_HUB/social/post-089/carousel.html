Slide 2 — What Are Zones?
Two Types of Zones
Demand Zone
Where buyers overwhelmed sellers
Strong rally originated here
Price returns to "refill" orders
Supply Zone
Where sellers overwhelmed buyers
Sharp decline started here
Unfilled orders wait for retest

Zones are areas, not single lines.

Slide 3 — S&R vs S&D
Support/Resistance vs. Supply/Demand
S & R
✕ Single price line
✕ Based on visible touches
✕ Easily hunted by spikes
✕ Reactive — drawn after the fact
S & D
✓ Price zone / area
✓ Based on order flow origin
✓ Absorbs wick noise
✓ Proactive — marks unfilled orders

Lines get hunted. Zones get respected.

Signal Pilot
Slide 4 — How to Spot Them
How to Identify a Zone
1
FIND THE IMPULSE
Look for a strong, aggressive move away from an area with large-body candles.
2
MARK THE BASE
The consolidation or single candle before the impulse is your zone boundary.
3
DRAW THE ZONE
Extend a rectangle from the base candle's high to its low (body or wick).
4
WAIT FOR RETEST
Trade the zone only when price returns to it with confirmation — not before.

Slide 5 — Key Truth
Fresh Zones Are Strongest.
Untested zones hold more unfilled orders
Each retest weakens the zone as orders get absorbed
The stronger the initial impulse, the better the zone
Higher timeframe zones overrule lower timeframe zones

Think in zones, not lines. Trade the area, not the pip.
