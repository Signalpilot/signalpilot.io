Slide 2 — FVG Types

Two Types of FVG

▲
Bullish FVG
Gap between candle 1 high and candle 3 low after an impulsive move up
Buy zone on retrace
▼
Bearish FVG
Gap between candle 1 low and candle 3 high after an impulsive move down
Sell zone on retrace

The gap exists because price moved with such conviction that no trading occurred in that zone.

Slide 4 — Pro Tips

Five FVG Pro Tips

H
Higher TF Priority
Higher timeframe FVGs carry more weight — a daily gap is stronger than a 15-minute gap
P
Partial Fills
Not all gaps fill fully — in strong trends, price may only tap the 50% level (consequent encroachment)
C
Combine with Structure
FVGs at order blocks or supply/demand zones are the highest probability setups
I
Watch for Inversions
A filled bullish FVG that fails to hold becomes bearish resistance — flip the bias
V
Volume Matters
Gaps created with high volume are more likely to act as price magnets on retrace

Slide 5 — Truth
F

The Truth About FVGs

Not All Gaps Fill
Strong trends leave unfilled gaps — forcing a fill trade is counter-trend trading in disguise
Context Is King
An FVG without confluence is just a zone on a chart — combine with structure for edge
Timeframe Alignment
A gap on the daily that aligns with 4H structure is worth ten gaps on the 5-minute
Patience Over Speed
Wait for price to come to the gap — chasing price to the gap defeats the purpose
"Fair value is where the market believes price should be — gaps reveal where it hasn't yet agreed."
